## Candidate paragraph (207 words)

The proposed thesis investigates whether an existing image can be marked through an image-conditioned diffusion noise path while remaining verifiable through a blind, image-domain DCT test. Its planned signature binds a public OwnerID to two content descriptions—a quantized semantic representation and a DCT-based perceptual hash—and its detector recomputes candidate signatures from the suspect image without inversion or an enrollment image. These elements form a falsifiable *combination*, not a demonstrated new capability. ZoDiac already optimizes diffusion latents for existing images and evaluates both photographs and generated images; SEAL derives semantic watermark information and uses inversion; SemBind and SLICE also bind diffusion marks to semantics; earlier image-DCT watermarking exists. Accordingly, neither latent embedding, semantic binding, DCT watermarking, nor real/generated coverage alone is claimed as original. The narrower proposed contribution is to test whether the two-component public content binding survives the latent-to-pixel bridge well enough for an inversion-free DCT verifier to distinguish valid marked instances from wrong-owner, unmarked and copied-mark controls at a declared quality and runtime budget. This claim fails if source-to-output signatures drift, C1 cannot separate from matched C0/C2, copying is falsely attributed, or full-pipeline verification is not advantageous under fair comparator access. Public OwnerID does not authenticate a person or prove legal ownership.
