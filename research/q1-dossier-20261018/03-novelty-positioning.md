# Novelty and article positioning

Prepared 2026-10-18. WP3 author assessment, not the WP9 independent review. Research only: no new experiment, image inspection, threshold adjustment or execution approval. The [evidence ledger](01-evidence-ledger.md) and [registry M2 review](01b-heldout-registry-m2-review.md) override the numerical shorthand in the original brief and MAIN state.

## Decision

**Primary framing: C, an empirical study of the boundary between regeneration recovery and registry attribution, using the pointer-assisted system as the principal case.** The measured separation is that the owner-conditioned gate excludes the passive competitor in the observed queries, while the content check fails on some operationally different look-alikes and stronger further regeneration produces substantial abstention. The WP9 saved-output check shows that beta's native owner detection already fails before q or descriptors are consulted (EV-77). The present comparison therefore does not isolate a content-binding or record-code contribution. This is a scoped empirical case, not a new hybrid architecture or a demonstrated secure ownership mechanism.

**Fallback framing: A, a narrower applied evaluation of pointer-assisted attribution under passive regenerate-and-enroll.** This is appropriate if later matched comparisons establish a useful operating point but do not establish a general boundary across methods. It must retain the E2 failure, trusted registry, missingness and attack-access restrictions. It is not a route to a stronger security headline with fewer tests. Framing B, a carrier-method article, should not lead this manuscript on present evidence.

**Q1 readiness: not ready for submission on writing changes alone.** C offers the strongest research question, but its present evidence concerns one selected system and one film's hard negatives. Matched external controls and fresh, diverse hard negatives are submission-critical for a general empirical claim. Independent-encoder evidence is needed to broaden the regeneration conclusion; an active re-marking evaluation is needed before extending the passive-enrollment security claim. These are proposals for later authorization, not work authorized in this session. No numerical probability of acceptance is justified.

## 1. Evidence and novelty boundaries

The local system marks existing photographs. F5 r2 changes RGB pixels using gradients through the pinned SD1.5 VAE encoder; its native extraction requires the model/profile information. It is an image-domain writer with an encoder-space objective, not insertion into the original photograph's generation latent and not a standalone lightweight DCT extractor. A known encoder at writing time is a capability, not evidence of encoder-independent robustness. The [Q-VAE finding](02-literature/Q-VAE.md) supports the phrase **unseen diffusion checkpoint** for SD2.1-base; shared encoder ancestry is material, and distinct locally pinned encoder tensors have not been established.

The registry amendment adds trusted side information: owner/record identifiers, carried code q, and continuous descriptors of the marked reference. D1 is the native fine-detail vector; B1 is the centered 32×32 gray-layout vector. They are not CLIP and pHash. The frozen rule requires native owner detection and code recovery, q within radius 6 of the record, then D1 ≥ .0594367 and B1 ≥ .90, or B1 ≥ .99 when D1 abstains. The different-image eligibility rule is gray PSNR <18.126 dB; it is an operational definition, not a human or legal identity judgment (EV-72; [approved plan](C:/Users/Soroush/.codex/worktrees/claude-registry-research/research/claude-registry-confirmation-plan-20261017.md), §2).

The source base contains 79 unique V2 works, including 77 papers and two standards/guidance documents, as documented in the [matrix](02-literature/matrix.csv). V2 means inspected methods and evaluation, not reproduction or peer-review endorsement. The comparisons below use the inspected versions and [WP2 synthesis](02-literature/synthesis.md). Consequential inaccessible papers remain in [unverified leads](02-literature/unverified-leads.md), especially the VAE-removal TIP paper, ConZWNet and Rad-Mark. They cannot support a positive or negative method comparison. No exhaustive priority claim is made.

## 2. Candidate contributions and closest V2 prior work

Risk means the risk that a reviewer rejects the proposed *novelty*, separately from whether the retained measurements are valid. Each candidate below has at least two inspected closest works. Missing evaluations in a cited version mean only that those evaluations were not established by the inspected sections.

### C1. Observed gating difference under passive competing enrollment

**Candidate claim.** In the frozen registry experiment, the full owner-conditioned pointer gate changes the accepted-owner set relative to the same descriptor-only arm (EV-58–66/75). A post-hoc count of saved outputs finds no successful beta native detection in any evaluable cell (EV-77), so this negative-owner difference is already implied by the first gate. It does not measure incremental rejection from q matching or content verification. Record correctness and owner correctness require separate controls before attributing a benefit to those stages.

| Closest V2 work | Exact difference in setting, access, side information and guarantee |
|---|---|
| [DREW](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02836v2), `saberi2024drew`, §§3.3–4.2 and Appendix C | Existing-image watermark routes to a database cluster, then embeddings retrieve; unreliable ECC can fall back to the full bank. It already evaluates diffusion purification. F5 instead uses an owner/record candidate and conjunctive record check, with passive competing enrollment as the local endpoint. Both need stored descriptors and decoding information. DREW's retrieval analysis is not a theorem about the thesis's attacker record acceptance; the local result is conditional empirical evidence. |
| [TrustMark final ICCV2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Bui_TrustMark_Robust_Watermarking_and_Watermark_Removal_for_Arbitrary_Resolution_Images_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf), `Bui2025TrustMark`, §4.5 p.18636 | Public post-hoc decoding retrieves metadata; the paper explicitly discusses checking a recovered fingerprint or thumbnail against the image to address identifier spoofing. This pre-empts pointer-plus-record architecture novelty. The local distinction is its specified regeneration/competing-enrollment evaluation, not inventing that check. Neither this passage nor the local passive test establishes security against arbitrary active re-marking. |
| [SynthID-Image](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09263v1), `gowal2025synthidimage`, §7 and §§8.1–8.2 | Explicit correct-payload AND stored-embedding matching is prior art. Service/model access restrictions differ from the local public-profile setting. Its inspected hybrid discussion is not a measured end-to-end competing-enrollment cascade. The local empirical comparison supplies a narrower result under declared records and access, not a novel conjunctive rule or transferable service-level guarantee. |
| [Roy and Chang](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~changec/publications/2004_Retrieval.pdf), `roy2004retrieval`, manuscript §§1–5 | Physical marking plus a stored index/message table already addresses ambiguity when similar content maps to different messages. Its fixed database and AWGN evaluation exclude association security. The local contribution changes the tested channel and registration scenario; it cannot claim to discover the general disambiguation rationale. |

**Novelty risk: high for architecture and for this result alone.** The comparison removes the whole gate within one system, and the passive competitor carries no beta mark. EV-77 shows why an ordinary owner detector can explain its zero-beta result. It is neither a component-identifying study nor a matched external hybrid baseline. Nor is q a demonstrated globally unique random record identifier: it is inherited content-derived code used to select candidates. Registry-scale uniqueness and collision behavior require their own analysis.

**Endpoint semantics.** The error predicate is any accepted attacker record, including an ambiguous original-plus-attacker owner set. Fingerprint-only any-attacker counts are 294/294, 293/294, 294/294, 263/293, 297/297, 296/297, 297/297 and 265/297 in the ledger's cell order (EV-75). Unique-attacker decisions range from 1 to 75 (EV-59–66); ambiguity is not a forced unique assignment. The headline “zero versus up to 75” conceals the declared endpoint and must not be used alone.

### C2. A measured specificity failure despite strong unrelated-image rejection

**Candidate claim.** The retained D1+B1 verifier fails its frozen look-alike endpoint even while available unrelated and transfer panels show zero accepts. This demonstrates a failure of this verifier under its operational same-shot identity criterion (EV-53–57/71/72), not the impossibility of robust instance verification.

| Closest V2 work | Exact difference in setting, access, side information and guarantee |
|---|---|
| [Soft-binding benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04151v1), `kazempourradi2026softbindings`, §§3.3, 5.2, 5.6, 6–7 | Already evaluates fingerprints, watermarks, near-copy false bindings and hybrid complementarity with registered descriptors and calibrated thresholds. Its watermark track excludes regeneration; fingerprint adversarial access is assessed separately. The local difference is a frozen regeneration-relative identity cutoff and passive competing enrollment with D1+B1. Neither a pair-level FPR target there nor local millions of comparisons certifies query-bank attribution risk. |
| [Rel-Zero](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17531v2), `chen2026relzero`, §§4–5 and Appendices F/G | Registers stable patch-pair relations without changing pixels, using a learned predictor, ViT and stored edge set. It evaluates generative edits/regeneration and unrelated collisions; its nominal FPR has a modeled null. F5 adds a physical pointer but uses different reference descriptors. The local same-shot negative failures and competing-owner outcomes are not established by Rel-Zero's inspected evaluation; neither system thereby proves legal ownership. |
| [SSCD](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.10261v2), `pizzi2022sscd`, §§2–5.4 | Existing-image copy retrieval uses a public learned descriptor and references, optionally background normalization. It explicitly separates shared-source copies from nearby video frames. Local D1+B1 is not SSCD, and the local regeneration-relative PSNR cutoff is a different endpoint. SSCD's copy-ranking evidence supplies no matched same-shot/regeneration guarantee, but makes it a principled future control. |

**Novelty risk: medium-to-high.** Near-copy failure itself is already known; the useful addition is its joint measurement with pointer recovery, owner-set ambiguity and abstention under one frozen channel. A single film and 16 eligible shots cannot establish a general specificity law. A stronger verifier is a future hypothesis, not a demonstrated remedy; geometric verification is not guaranteed to improve the trade-off by the soft-binding evidence.

### C3. An encoder-objective image writer with measured recovery in a fixed channel

**Candidate claim.** F5 implements per-image RGB optimization through a known encoder and shows descriptive gains over its retained image-domain comparator (EV-10–12), followed by held-out registry attribution with additional side information (EV-50/51). This supports a reproducible implementation and observed behavior, not causal proof of an encoder-amplification mechanism.

| Closest V2 work | Exact difference in setting, access, side information and guarantee |
|---|---|
| [FreqMark](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20824v1), `Guo2024FreqMark`, §§4–5 | Optimizes frequency perturbations of an encoded VAE latent, decodes the image and extracts through fixed DINOv2 feature directions. F5 optimizes image pixels against encoder-space DCT chip objectives. Both use frozen models and per-image optimization; F5's detector needs its pinned encoder/profile, and the registry version additionally needs records. Regeneration bit recovery is not a matched attribution guarantee. |
| [SimuFreeMark](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11295v1), `tang2025simufreemark`, §§3–4 | Processes an existing image's low frequencies through a frozen VAE, inserts a learned latent residual, restores high frequencies and uses a learned image decoder. F5's optimized variable and objective differ; both exploit VAE structure. Its bit-accuracy editing evidence and F5's attribution evidence use different metrics and information. The original proposal's generic latent-insertion/inversion-free premise is already crowded. |
| [RoSteALS](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03400v1), `Bui2023RoSteALS`, §§3–4 | Learned content-independent latent offsets go through a frozen autoencoder; a learned image decoder reads the message. The inspected suite is conventional corruption, not matched regeneration. F5 uses encoder-directed pixel optimization rather than a learned latent-offset path. This is an algorithmic distinction requiring controlled evidence, not a new use of autoencoders or provenance identifiers. |
| [VINE](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18775v2), `Lu2025VINE`, §§3–4 | A generative watermark encoder and trained extractor use staged training and editing surrogates; the stronger variant includes an editing model during training. F5 uses per-image optimization through a pinned encoder. Training access, detector weights and cost differ. VINE supplies calibrated editing/regeneration detection results, so generic diffusion robustness is not a new claim and raw reported numbers cannot establish F5 superiority. |

**Novelty risk: high.** A narrow objective/optimization distinction exists, but the ledger does not supply a matched mechanism-isolating ablation, a modern matched external method comparison or independent-encoder transfer. Known-encoder sensitivity is a plausible explanation, not a demonstrated general principle. The retained WAM development comparison (EV-43) has payload, detector and threshold differences and cannot fill this gap. Closed FreqMark retries and other closed method lines remain closed; no local rerun is proposed as an authorized action.

### C4. An auditable negative confirmation separating recovery, binding and availability

**Candidate claim.** The completed protocol supplies a reproducible case in which a regeneration recovery endpoint passes while the full attribution confirmation fails, and complete-case reporting differs from strict bank completeness (EV-50–75). Separating these outcomes improves the scientific interpretation of this case.

| Closest V2 work | Exact difference in setting, access, side information and guarantee |
|---|---|
| [WAVES](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08573v3), `an2024waves`, §§3–4 and Appendices E/F/G | Already separates detection, user identification, attack quality and encoder access over several watermark systems. Its candidate-user identification task does not supply the local trusted-record completeness or D1+B1 hard-negative result. The local study contributes endpoint-specific evidence, not a new idea that detection differs from identification. |
| [Craver et al.](https://web.cs.ucla.edu/~miodrag/cs259-security/craver98resolving.pdf), `craver1998ownership`, §§IV–VIII | Constructs competing ownership evidence with claimed originals/marks under scheme-specific access, including inverse and quasi-inverse ambiguity. | Soft-binding benchmark, `kazempourradi2026softbindings`, §§5–7 | Already treats watermark success, retrieval, false binding and combination as separate quantities. The local added axis is bank completeness and source-majority regeneration attribution with observed abstention. **Novelty risk: high as a standalone methods contribution; moderate as the organizing empirical contribution.** Good accounting does not by itself make a Q1 paper. The article needs an externally relevant question and matched comparisons showing when the measured separation matters beyond this implementation.

## 4. Contribution statement for the manuscript

We study the distinction between watermark recovery and registered-image attribution under diffusion regeneration, using an image-domain F5 carrier and a trusted registry of native detail and layout descriptors. On a frozen held-out protocol, source-majority attribution at strength .2 succeeds for 277/300 SD1.5 and 280/300 SD2.1-base source groups, with unavailable outputs counted as misses (EV-50/51). Under the approved pre-result complete-case reading, the pointer arm accepts no attacker record in eight passive regenerate-and-enroll cells, while fingerprint-only matching frequently includes the competing owner; incomplete banks and stronger-regeneration abstention limit this result (EV-58–66/75). The overall confirmation fails because the descriptor check accepts 3/45 operationally different real look-alike pairs from 2/16 shots (EV-55/70/71). Together these observations characterize a bounded trade-off among recovery, disambiguation and instance specificity; they establish neither a new hybrid architecture nor active-forgery security.

### Contribution bullets

- **A frozen attribution case study:** measure source-level registry attribution for existing photographs under two specified diffusion checkpoints, while declaring the encoder/model and enrolled descriptors as side information (EV-50–52/72).
- **A scoped whole-gate-versus-fingerprint comparison:** report original-owner acceptance, any accepted attacker, ambiguity and abstention under passive enrollment, retaining incomplete banks and the owner-gate explanation (EV-58–66/75/77). Do not present this as a causal isolation of q or content binding.
- **A substantive binding failure:** document same-shot negatives that defeat the frozen descriptor rule despite zero accepts in the available unrelated/transfer panels, with source/shot dependence and missingness explicit (EV-53–57/71).
- **A bounded interpretation of the failed confirmation:** preserve the frozen verdict and availability amendment, distinguish image-domain carrier evidence from the proposal's unfulfilled latent-insertion/lightweight-extraction objectives, and identify claims that require fresh evidence (EV-70; Q-VAE).

These are proposed empirical contributions. The fourth is a reporting/interpretation contribution, not a novel estimator; none implies that the manuscript is submission-ready. In the contribution paragraph above, the E3 difference describes the whole pipeline only: EV-77 identifies failed beta owner detection as a sufficient explanation for observed beta rejection. Future F1 must separate owner, q and content checks and preserve native-versus-adapted external rules before claiming additional component value.

## 5. Must not claim

1. First watermark identifier, first registry watermark, first watermark/fingerprint conjunction, first hybrid under regeneration, or first physical mark to disambiguate registered content. DREW, final TrustMark, SynthID-Image, Roy/Chang and the soft-binding benchmark are direct antecedents.
2. First VAE-aware, latent-frequency, inversion-free or diffusion-robust existing-photo watermark. FreqMark, SimuFreeMark, RoSteALS and VINE substantially occupy that space; unresolved journal sources further limit priority claims.
3. That F5 fulfills the original latent-insertion or lightweight DCT-detector objectives, or that D1+B1 is a CLIP/pHash signature.
4. Independent-autoencoder robustness from SD2.1-base, universal diffusion robustness, or a causal proof of encoder amplification from whole-system gains.
5. A successful overall confirmation, an unqualified E3 pass, or zero errors for every scheduled negative/query-bank case.
6. Zero false attribution certified by millions of independent trials. The scored pair count reuses sources and records and omits unavailable comparisons.
7. “Fingerprint makes up to 75 errors” as the whole E3 comparison. Unique wrong-owner decisions and ambiguity are different outcomes; the declared any-attacker predicate includes both.
8. Active re-marking, adaptive querying, public-writer forgery or general residual-transplant security from passive regenerate-and-enroll. Those adversarial capabilities are not interchangeable.
9. That the three descriptor-only accepts demonstrate three successful whole-system pointer forgeries, or that missing pointer candidates excuse the frozen descriptor endpoint failure.
10. Cryptographic unforgeability, authenticated or honest enrollment, legal ownership, first creation, or trustworthiness of a registry merely because a record exists. Dynamic ownership, transfers and ledgers remain outside thesis scope.
11. Human-validated identity or perceptual equivalence, or a human quality-rating study. No such held-out ratings exist. Quality thresholds are not satisfied on all attempted registry marks (EV-73/74).
12. State-of-the-art accuracy, lower latency, superiority at matched FPR, or scalability beyond the evaluated bank from cross-paper tables or cached lookup durations.
13. A comprehensive absence of prior art when consequential papers remain V1/V0, or a verified Q1 venue/acceptance probability before WP4 and the university ranking decision.
