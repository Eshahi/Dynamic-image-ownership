# NIST AI 100-4 — transparency evaluation guidance

- **Title:** Reducing Risks Posed by Synthetic Content: An Overview of Technical Approaches to Digital Content Transparency.
- **Authors:** Bilva Chandra; Jesse Dunietz; Kathleen Roberts; Yooyoung Lee; Peter Fontana; George Awad (official publication metadata).
- **Year/version:** November 2024 final report, NIST AI 100-4; approved 18 November, published 20 November.
- **URL/DOI:** https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-4.pdf ; 10.6028/NIST.AI.100-4.
- **Access/verification:** 2026-10-18; V2 **technical guidance**, substantive mechanism and evaluation-framework inspection, not a new experiment.
- **Locators:** §3, especially §3.1.1 (printed pp.5–7); §§4.1.1–4.3 (printed pp.32–34; PDF pp.37–39 zero-based).

**Direct evidence.** Watermarking and signed metadata are complementary transparency tools. Robustness to ordinary changes and security against malicious removal/forgery are separate evaluation dimensions. Evaluation should account for content distributions, unfamiliar generators, attack conditions and distortion. Low-FPR operating-point reporting can be more informative than aggregate accuracy. Content provenance does not by itself establish trustworthiness. Payload, keys, originals and external repositories depend on the method; the report imposes no universal configuration. There is no single dataset, sample count or new numerical benchmark to transfer to the thesis.

**Inference/relevance.** Supports explicit threat/access statements and separate false-attribution endpoints. It does not certify the thesis, establish novelty or supply a Q1 publication gate.
