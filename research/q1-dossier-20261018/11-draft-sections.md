## 1. Introduction

Diffusion regeneration can preserve much of a photograph's visible content while changing the signal that an invisible watermark detector observes. This creates an apparent engineering objective: retain a detectable mark while limiting distortion. Yet successful signal recovery answers only part of an attribution question. A recovered owner identifier does not by itself show that the query corresponds to the correct image registered by that owner. Conversely, a perceptual match may connect several records, including records submitted by different claimants. A useful evaluation must therefore distinguish recovery, record specificity, conflicting attribution and failure to return an attribution.

These distinctions are especially relevant for existing photographs. Unlike a system that controls image synthesis, a post-hoc writer receives an image whose production process is already complete. It can modify that image and publish associated metadata, but it cannot retrospectively insert a mark into its original generation trajectory. Learned post-hoc watermarks and encoder-aware constructions already address substantial parts of this problem [@Tancik2020StegaStamp; @Lu2025VINE; @Guo2024FreqMark]. Their robustness motivates examining the attribution decisions made after recovery, rather than treating a recovered payload as the final evidence of a content-specific claim.

Registry assistance is an established approach to this second stage. Watermark information can point to stored material, narrow a retrieval search or be checked against an enrolled content representation. DREW combines watermark routing with embedding retrieval; the published TrustMark work discusses recovered fingerprints or thumbnails; and SynthID-Image describes jointly requiring an appropriate payload and stored-embedding agreement [@saberi2024drew; @Bui2025TrustMark; @gowal2025synthidimage]. We consequently make no architectural priority claim for combining a watermark with a record. The research question is narrower: which attribution events does a particular frozen hybrid system preserve or confuse under regeneration and passive competing enrollment?

We study an image-domain writer whose optimization objective is expressed through a pinned image encoder. Its public owner-conditioned signal carries a content-derived code. A trusted registry stores that code and continuous descriptors of the marked, published image. Verification first checks the owner-conditioned signal and code eligibility, then applies a frozen descriptor rule. The output is a set of accepted records and owners, allowing simultaneous claims to remain visible. The registry is additional detector side information; its integrity is assumed, while truthful authorship and legal ownership are not inferred from registration.

The frozen confirmation separates three questions. E1 measures recovery of the intended registered record after a specified regeneration channel. E2 stress-tests the descriptor check on unrelated, transferred and real look-alike content. E3 compares the complete pointer-assisted decision with a descriptor-only decision when a second claimant enrolls a regenerated copy without inserting a new mark. This last restriction is material: it measures passive competing enrollment, not security against the publicly available writer or an adaptive attacker. We retain unavailable results and ambiguous owner sets in the interpretation rather than treating them as successful rejection.

The observed results expose different limits. On the held-out cohort, source-majority recovery at strength .2 was 277/300 with SD1.5 and 280/300 with SD2.1-base (EV-50–51). Nevertheless, the descriptor rule accepted three operationally different real pairs from two shots, causing the overall confirmation to fail (EV-55/70/71). In the available E3 cells, the pointer-assisted rule accepted no beta record, but a post-hoc count shows that beta had already failed the native owner gate in every completed query (EV-58–66/77). This contrast therefore cannot identify additional rejection supplied by the record code or descriptor check. Stronger further regeneration also substantially increased abstention (EV-62/66).

The study's contribution is a bounded account of these distinct events in a frozen system, including negative specificity evidence and the limits of the component comparison. It is not a proof that hybrid attribution is generally secure or superior. Matched controls that separate owner, code and content stages, together with diverse fresh hard negatives, are still needed to assess a generalizable contribution. The present sections provide the supported case study and the explicit question such a subsequent study would test; they do not report the proposed experiments as completed.

## 2. Related Work

Watermark recovery and image attribution ask related but different questions. A detector may establish that a signal is present, recover a payload, or associate a query with stored content. These outcomes depend on distinct side information and error events. For existing photographs, a post-hoc writer changes an already available image; a generation-time system instead controls some part of synthesis. This distinction matters when interpreting robustness, training access and detector cost. Our study concerns post-hoc images and a declared registry, and therefore does not treat all watermarking results as interchangeable attribution evidence.

Post-hoc learned systems already provide substantial robustness and payload functionality. StegaStamp encodes identifiers for photographs subject to physical recapture [@Tancik2020StegaStamp]. RoSteALS uses a frozen autoencoder with learned latent offsets and an image decoder [@Bui2023RoSteALS]. VINE uses generative priors and staged training to address image editing, with evaluation designed around that setting [@Lu2025VINE]. Watermark Anything extends watermarking toward localization and multiple regions [@Sander2025WAM]. These systems establish that learned visual channels and useful identifiers precede the evaluated carrier. Their bit or detection metrics, however, do not directly estimate acceptance of a competing registered record under our enrollment procedure.

VAE-aware construction is also established prior art. FreqMark optimizes frequency perturbations of an encoded latent and extracts using fixed feature directions [@Guo2024FreqMark]. SimuFreeMark processes low-frequency image content through a frozen VAE, inserts a learned latent residual and restores high-frequency content before image-domain decoding [@tang2025simufreemark]. These mechanisms differ from optimizing RGB pixels against an encoder-space objective, but the difference alone does not demonstrate a superior or more general principle. A method-level claim would require controlled comparisons and causal evidence. We instead treat the encoder-directed writer as the evaluated component of a broader attribution experiment.

Generation-time watermarks set a different boundary. Tree-Ring and Gaussian Shading organize initial noise or latent distributions, while Stable Signature and AquaLoRA modify components of the generative pipeline [@wen2023treering; @yang2024gaussianshading; @fernandez2023stable; @feng2024aqualora]. Their assumptions about model control, inversion or decoder access cannot be transferred without qualification to arbitrary existing photographs. Nor does using DCT features somewhere in a model-dependent pipeline establish a lightweight, model-free detector. We distinguish the writer's optimized variable, its objective space and the verifier's computational dependencies throughout.

Regeneration attacks show why recovery requires its own evaluation. Zhao et al. analyze removal through noisy representations and derive conditional detection limits [@zhao2024provablyremovable]. A bound becoming weak in a high-gain watermark direction does not prove survival through denoising. WAVES emphasizes evaluation across attack conditions, and CtrlRegen and UnMarker broaden the mechanisms and access models used to stress watermark robustness [@an2024waves; @liu2025ctrlregen; @kassis2025unmarker]. Such work motivates careful fidelity and attack-budget reporting. It does not make nominally equal diffusion strengths equivalent across models, or establish that two checkpoints with shared encoder ancestry provide independent-encoder evidence.

Registered content and hybrid verification are direct antecedents to our setting. Roy and Chang combine physical marking with stored index/message information [@roy2004retrieval]. DREW routes watermark information into database-assisted embedding retrieval and evaluates diffusion purification [@saberi2024drew]. The published TrustMark paper explicitly discusses checking a recovered fingerprint or thumbnail to address identifier spoofing [@Bui2025TrustMark]. SynthID-Image also describes acceptance based jointly on a correct payload and a stored embedding [@gowal2025synthidimage]. Consequently, neither an identifier linked to a record nor a watermark–fingerprint conjunction is our architectural contribution. Our narrower question is what a frozen system's accepted-owner sets reveal under a specified passive competing-enrollment procedure.

Zero-watermarking further limits broad novelty claims about content registration. It stores content-derived evidence without necessarily inserting a physical carrier. Rel-Zero, for example, registers stable patch relations and evaluates generative transformations, under its own representation and null-model assumptions [@chen2026relzero]. The distinction from a physical pointer is operational: pointer eligibility can restrict which records are considered, whereas a content-only matcher relies on the registered representation. Whether that restriction improves attribution at useful recovery is an empirical question. Registration itself neither establishes rightful creation nor guarantees separation of regenerated copies from visually similar, independently captured instances.

Copy detection provides relevant representations and challenging negatives. SSCD separates shared-source copies from nearby video frames, making it a principled reference for instance-level retrieval [@pizzi2022sscd]. DELG combines local and global features for retrieval [@cao2020delg], while the recent soft-binding benchmark examines watermark/fingerprint complementarity and false bindings to near copies [@kazempourradi2026softbindings]. These results motivate stronger controls, but do not guarantee that adding geometric verification improves a particular operating point. A benchmark's retrieval score, modeled false-positive threshold or oracle combination is not automatically the risk that any false record is accepted from a registry after candidate selection.

Attribution ambiguity and copy attacks also have a long history. Craver et al. analyze conflicting ownership claims and invertibility, and Kutter et al. study copying watermark evidence [@craver1998ownership; @kutter2000copy]. Modern diffusion-watermark forgery work demonstrates that attack access must be specified rather than inferred from a method's name [@muller2025forgery; @jain2025single]. Passive enrollment of a regenerated image does not give the attacker the same capabilities as active re-marking or detector-guided optimization. Our accepted-owner-set endpoint includes simultaneous acceptance of the original and competing owner, while distinguishing ambiguity from a unique wrong-owner assignment.

Finally, provenance standards and statistical practice constrain interpretation. C2PA's soft-binding framework and NIST's synthetic-content guidance situate watermarking within a larger evidence system [@c2pa24contentcredentials; @chandra2024nist1004]. They do not make a perceptual match a legal ownership judgment. Reusing sources, references and video shots creates dependence that a large pair count cannot remove; exact binomial intervals require a specified unit and sampling model [@brown2001binomial; @cameron2015cluster]. We therefore report recovery, specificity, ambiguity, abstention and unavailable outcomes separately. The intended contribution is a bounded empirical account of their interaction, with matched controls and broader hard-negative evidence identified as remaining requirements.

For future comparisons, the distinction between architecture and operating policy is consequential. DREW's native enrollment marks records, its query rule returns a top match or rejection, and unreliable watermark decoding can trigger global-bank retrieval. Our passive competing record is not re-marked, and our decision retains every accepted owner. A comparison must report native policies and any common-policy adaptation separately; forcing DREW to use a mandatory pointer or silently removing its fallback would change the evaluated method [@saberi2024drew]. This compatibility requirement is part of the prospective comparison contract, not a completed baseline result.

Recent work further differentiates the objects that a hybrid system links. NRGMark uses a decoded UUID to retrieve a C2PA manifest, whereas Signpost routes to watermark technologies rather than enrolled originals [@Agarwal2026NRGMark; @Agarwal2026Signpost]. Nemecek et al. examine inconsistencies between signed provenance assertions and watermark evidence under controlled signing conditions [@Nemecek2026IntegrityClash]. These antecedents reinforce the need to state the registry's role and trust assumptions precisely. Active overwrite and geometric regeneration supply distinct interventions: Watermarks Attack Watermarks separates replacement-payload recovery from victim detection, while RAVEN studies removal through latent transformations [@Bulychev2026WAW; @Shamshad2026RAVEN]. Their evaluations do not test our registry and cannot extend passive competing-enrollment results into active-forgery guarantees. We retain version-specific reading limits and avoid ranking incomparable endpoint metrics.
## 3. Setting and Threat Model

Let x denote an existing source image, a a public owner identifier, and w = W(x,a) the published marked image. An enrolling party submits a record associating a record identifier, a, a carried content code and reference descriptors of w. The registry preserves that association and reports record availability. Trust in these operations does not entail trust that the enrolling party created the image. A competing claimant may enroll a derived image under its own identifier while leaving the original record unchanged. Authentication of enrollment, priority adjudication and rights transfer are outside this evaluation.

The verifier is given the suspect image, the owner candidates, the fixed watermark profile and the registry. Native calls also depend on the pinned VAE and CLIP components. Thus the system is neither a reference-free instance verifier nor a lightweight DCT-only extractor. Public derivation of owner-specific patterns is not a secret possession test: the word keyed denotes pattern construction and must not be interpreted as cryptographic authentication. An accepted result is evidence of agreement under the declared computational rule, not a legal conclusion about ownership.

The defended threat scope has three parts. T3 is the protocol's nonadaptive diffusion-regeneration channel, defined by checkpoint, scheduler, prompt, guidance, strength and seeds. For the primary endpoint, source success means intended-record acceptance for a majority of the scheduled seeds; an unavailable outcome remains a miss. T4 transfers the residual between a marked donor and its clean version onto another recipient. It therefore grants clean-donor access and tests a particular residual-transfer construction, rather than all marked-image-only copying attacks. T5 examines confusion with distinct content, including unrelated records and same-shot look-alikes (EV-50–55/72).

Eligibility of a real look-alike negative follows the frozen gray-PSNR predicate, below 18.126065828471827 dB after canonicalization. This threshold was derived from a development regeneration percentile. It is an operational rule for this endpoint, not a perceptual equivalence relation or an assurance that every eligible pair has a different human identity. The source-preserving positive channel is defined by its transformation provenance. We do not apply the negative predicate after seeing E1 outcomes to discard difficult positive regenerations (EV-72).

Passive competing enrollment adds a second claimant beta. Starting with alpha's marked image, beta obtains the specified regeneration, computes the beta-conditioned record code and enrolls the derived content without inserting a beta watermark. The suspect is either that enrolled image or its scheduled further regeneration. Both the pointer-assisted and descriptor-only decisions operate on the corresponding saved record bank. A competing-attribution error occurs whenever any beta record is accepted, including when alpha is accepted simultaneously. The four owner-level outcomes are original only, beta only, both, and neither. Record correctness remains a separate question when multiple records belong to one owner.

Availability is part of this setting. A valid native descriptor abstention can invoke the rule's declared fallback, whereas a missing required artifact is an unknown comparison. The strict E3 bank-completeness condition failed in the retained run. Its approved pre-result complete-case analysis describes available queries and records conditionally; it does not prove what absent competing records would have done (EV-58–70). Missing cases cannot be counted as successful negatives merely because no acceptance is stored.

The evaluated adversary does not actively re-mark the regenerated image, optimize an image against the verifier, search with detector feedback or train a robustified encoder. These capabilities remain outside the measured claim even though public method information could permit broader actions. Correspondingly, a zero passive error count is not a cryptographic security guarantee. A meaningful soundness statement must specify the candidate bank, negative distribution, availability policy and useful recovery requirement; a system that rejects every query also has no competing attributions.

## 4. Evaluated System

### 4.1 Encoder-directed image-domain writer

F5 r2 optimizes the RGB image while evaluating its robust carrier through the pinned SD1.5 VAE encoder. The encoder's scaled posterior-mode latent is transformed using a channel-wise orthonormal DCT. A fixed radial coefficient band and public frequency weights define the slots assigned to owner-conditioned signed carrier chips. The optimization increases the prescribed chip margins using a squared-hinge objective, normalized gradient updates, a pixel-domain perturbation budget and box constraints. The image is quantized to RGB8 before verification. These operations describe optimization through an encoder, not direct insertion into the original image's latent generative history.

The retained construction also includes the predecessor's fragile luminance channel and a refinement stage. Their presence is part of the writer specification, but the registry endpoint does not require the predecessor's full semantic-and-fragile `both_match` result. Conflating that historical decision with native pointer eligibility would change the endpoint. The complete frozen profile and source-function locators are supplied in the reproducibility manifest; empirical quality is reported from final marked outputs, not inferred from an individual optimization budget (EV-72/73).

Encoder-directed optimization supplies a plausible mechanism for increasing a particular latent response at limited image distortion. It does not prove preservation by a diffusion denoiser or transfer to another encoder. The retained SD2.1-base result tests an unseen diffusion checkpoint, while the available architecture evidence indicates shared encoder ancestry with the SD1.x family. It should not be described as independent-encoder robustness. This restriction also prevents interpreting a loose removal bound as a positive robustness theorem.

### 4.2 Content code and enrollment

The semantic path computes a content-derived code using CLIP features and owner/profile-dependent projections. The revision used here aggregates the prescribed feature views before deriving the code. This code is neither a freshly drawn uniform database identifier nor a cryptographic digest of exact image bytes. Nearby images can have nearby codes, and owner conditioning does not create a secret where the profile derivation is public.

For each marked image w, enrollment stores a record r containing its record identifier, owner, code q_r, D1(w), B1(w) and version/profile metadata. D1 is the native fine-detail descriptor. B1 is a mean-centered, unit-normalized gray-layout vector constructed by BOX resizing to the frozen grid. These enrolled descriptors are additional side information. D1 is not CLIP and B1 is not pHash; the system must not be presented as fulfilling the proposal's original CLIP-plus-pHash hypothesis merely because CLIP is used elsewhere in code construction (EV-72; WP8).

The marked published image is the reference for alpha's record. In E3, beta instead registers its regenerated copy and recomputes the beta-conditioned code without embedding a beta carrier. That difference between physical marking and registration is essential to the interpretation of the owner-gate result.

### 4.3 Candidate eligibility and record verification

For a suspect y and owner a, the native semantic call reports `found`, `read` and a decoded code when available. Record r is pointer-eligible only if detection and reading both succeed and the Hamming distance from the decoded code to q_r is at most six:

`P_r(y) = found_a(y) AND read_a(y) AND [d_H(qhat_a(y), q_r) <= 6]`.

This is a set-valued lookup. Multiple records may satisfy the tolerance, so pointer eligibility must not be described as uniquely identifying a record. The registry does not replace a missing decoded code with a recomputed query code to force eligibility.

For available descriptors, let d_r be D1 cosine similarity and b_r be B1 cosine similarity. The frozen content predicate is `d_r >= .0594367 AND b_r >= .90`. When the native D1 computation validly abstains, the predicate instead requires `b_r >= .99`. A missing query or record artifact is unknown and does not qualify for that fallback (EV-72). The latter branch is a one-descriptor rule, so the method cannot claim that two descriptor checks always contribute to rejection.

The accepted record set is `M(y;R) = {r in R : P_r(y) AND F_r(y)}` and the accepted owner set is the owners represented in M. Every accepted record is retained; a displayed `best_record` is not an exclusive-winner decision. The descriptor-only arm uses the same records and content predicate while omitting P. It is a within-system comparison, not a substitute for an externally matched fingerprint baseline.

### 4.4 What the component comparison identifies

The saved beta native calls have `found=false` and `read=false` in every completed E3 query (EV-77). Thus no observed beta record reaches the code-distance or descriptor stages in the pointer-assisted arm. The absence of competing attribution is explained at the owner gate. The result does not identify the extra benefit of the code check, the descriptor check, or their conjunction, and it does not imply that these checks are unnecessary for alpha's correct-record identification.

A future component study would need owner-only, owner-plus-code, owner-plus-content and complete rules, with multiple distinct records per owner. Owner-only outputs would have no record-correctness endpoint. The preregistered question would be whether the code improves record discrimination beyond owner-plus-content while retaining useful recovery. This is the future F1 study specified in the gap plan; it has not been executed and is not a result of the present manuscript.
