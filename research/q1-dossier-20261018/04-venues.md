# Journal selection for the attribution-boundary article

WP4, checked **18 October 2026**, with a **19 October continuation**. Recommendation is conditional on additional scientific evidence and the university's ranking rule. No manuscript was submitted, editor contacted, fee paid or institutional decision made.

**Primary target: IEEE TIFS**, for an empirical security/forensics paper explaining when regenerated-image recovery does and does not support registered-image attribution. **Backups: IEEE TMM and ACM TOMM**, for a narrower multimedia-security evaluation. TIFS has the clearest subject fit and explicit publisher guidance addressing negative results; it is also the most demanding of these choices on the current evidence. This is a research destination, not a recommendation to submit now. Writing alone cannot close the missing matched controls, diverse hard negatives and attack-access limitations identified in [WP3](03-novelty-positioning.md).

The ranking evidence is deliberately uneven. TIFS now has **direct IEEE-publisher Q1 evidence for JCR 2024, released June 2025**, from the January 2026 title list; that table supplies no category. The older agency reproduction corroborates two categories, but neither source verifies TIFS's newest June 2026 category rows. Its 2024 SCImago best-Q1 remains separately supported. TMM's current named-category Q1 is a calculation from publisher rank/total reports, and TOMM's Q1 is explicit in ACM's report. Obtain the institution-required edition/category evidence before treating any venue as eligible. Journal reputation, impact factor, CiteScore and a paper's merit are separate matters.

## Format, costs and reproducibility consequences

For the proposed primary route, plan a regular **TIFS paper within 13 initial two-column pages**, with a concise main narrative and reproducibility supplement. The [SPS rules](https://signalprocessingsociety.org/publications-resources/information-authors) allow 16 revised pages, but published pages beyond ten incur charges. The [2026 IEEE fee list](https://magazines.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/IEEE-Article-Processing-Charges-List.pdf) distinguishes optional OA **USD 2,800** from mandatory **USD 220 per overlength page**. Traditional publication avoids the OA APC, not overlength charges. These are present policy facts, not a spending authorization or a quote for a later year.

TMM has tighter initial/revised limits of **10/14 pages**, a four-page supplementary cap, and its overlength threshold starts after eight pages. A TMM version needs a shorter theory/main-method section. TIP shares the SPS 13/16-page caps and ten-page no-overlength threshold. TCSVT's current CASS page gives a 14-page cap; the dated IEEE fee list gives USD 125/page beyond eleven, while the undated CASS page gives different member/nonmember amounts. That conflict is retained, not resolved by guessing.

For TOMM, the accessible author guide is visibly legacy; **23+5 pages is historical guidance only**. ACM's full-OA policy and charges are current and verified separately. A 2027 article may face **USD 1,800 / 1,300 member** list pricing rather than 2026's temporary USD 1,450 / 950 subsidy; qualifying institutional coverage can remove the author APC. Institution eligibility is unknown. This article's need for more research makes 2027 pricing relevant to planning.

Elsevier's accessible descriptions verify **30 single-column, double-spaced pages** for Signal Processing original research and **20–35** for Pattern Recognition research articles. Other current caps and title-specific APCs/anonymization/decision times were blocked or unverified, as the fact sheets state. They are not treated as unlimited, free or rapid. No current journal-level editorial turnaround is promised for any shortlisted destination. First-decision statistics, where later obtained, must not be read as external-review duration.

The [TIFS deep-learning guidance](https://signalprocessingsociety.org/sites/default/files/uploads/publications_resources/docs/Guidelines__deep_learning_submissions.pdf) is particularly relevant: its encouragement of negative findings specifically concerns contradictions of previously published research and imposes higher reproducibility requirements. Failure of this project's own verifier is not automatically such a contribution or evidence of editorial preference for this case. The manuscript needs exact detector side information, development/held-out separation, frozen thresholds, unavailability accounting, legal artifact availability and an externally relevant comparison. General Elsevier research-data guidance and ACM supplemental options do not establish universal mandatory public-code policies; current journal-specific rules must be checked at submission.

## Related-paper fit is not a scientific comparison

The fact sheets contain at least two DOI-linked 2024–2026 publications per screened title. Examples closest to this project include TIFS's *Dual Defense* and *MantleMark*; TMM's *MarkPlugger*; TOMM's *FaceSigns* and contrastive medical zero-watermarking; JISA's *ConZWNet* and STDM/latent diffusion watermarking; and TIP's VAE-removal-resistant reversible watermarking. Several are available only at V1 in this session. Their publication proves a relevant journal neighborhood, not method equivalence, superior accuracy or that a new submission will be accepted.

DOI strings may contain the online-registration year rather than issue year. The fact sheets preserve, for example, TIFS MantleMark's 2025 DOI/2026 volume and Information Sciences' conditional watermarking 2025 DOI/June 2026 issue. No future-dated issue is represented as a current published experiment without an established publication date.

## Special issues and exclusions

No directly matching, still-open official call was verified. Current TCSVT calls on spatial intelligence, large-model compression and AV2 are not a reason to reshape this still-image attribution paper. Pattern Recognition's indexed future Security and Trustworthiness section is a collection/issue lead, not an established open deadline. An encountered TOMM multimodal-fusion deadline already passed. The default is a regular article.

No predatory or borderline outlet is recommended. The screen uses established publisher journal identities, exact titles/ISSNs and primary pages. Lookalike domains `computersandsecurity.org` and `sciencedirectelsevier.com` are excluded as evidence. Computers & Security itself is a legitimate journal; its exclusion here is entirely about the **current subject scope**.
