## D. Ranked future experiments — NOT RUN

### F1. Matched external control for the empirical attribution claim — submission-critical

**Hypothesis:** the joint pattern of recovery, false binding, competing-owner ambiguity and abstention contains an informative difference from an established fingerprint/hybrid control, rather than only a consequence of this system's weak descriptor or threshold.

**Design:** propose F5+registry, the identical frozen descriptor-only arm, an SSCD reference arm, and one externally documented hybrid (DREW preferred if its implementation/profile can be vetted). This is a small comparison, not a new baseline sweep. Use the same 300 fresh photographs, same source groups, two fixed regeneration checkpoints at .2 with three seeds, and the F2 hard negatives; retain clean/unmarked/wrong-owner controls. Match input information, registry size, payload/candidate semantics where meaningful, quality budget and calibration data. If a method cannot be matched, document that difference rather than normalize away its cost. Thresholds come only from separate development data; do not calibrate to held-out zero errors. Report record acceptance, any-beta acceptance, ambiguity and abstention, with confidence intervals on appropriate groups.

**Success:** a prespecified practically meaningful trade-off or reproducible cross-method boundary remains on fresh groups; a positive difference is not required for an honest empirical paper. A provisional comparison margin is 5 percentage points in recovery at a fixed development-calibrated false-binding operating point; its suitability and power require a later protocol. **Kill:** external controls explain away the proposed distinctive finding, or the study cannot fairly align access/quality/operating points. Then use the narrower case-study framing and do not claim a general boundary.

**Data/cost:** Open Images and F2 groups; 3–7 researcher-days; laptop 24–72 h or rented 24–72 h (**USD 21.36–64.08** compute allowance). This does not promise DREW training-free compatibility. **Unlocks:** controlled comparative attribution evidence. **Authorization dependency:** new external baseline work is currently closed and must be explicitly reopened.

### F2. Fresh, diverse hard negatives and complete-bank confirmation — submission-critical

**Hypothesis:** the frozen D1+B1 specificity failure and pointer/descriptor distinction persist beyond one film; the complete-bank version of E3 has the claimed operating point.

**Design:** freeze the existing method unchanged; acquire approximately 100 independent source groups across at least 20 creators/productions, with three temporal pairs per group, and 300 fresh ordinary photographs. Keep a distinct development pool for logistics only; no tuning on this confirmation. Evaluate both the descriptor-only negative endpoint and the full pointer-plus-record endpoint, passive enrollment with complete banks, and accepted-owner-set outcomes. Record every attempted source and missing comparison. Use both original operational labels and prospective blinded identity labels if approved; disagreements remain visible. Primary claim is source-group risk, with creator/video dependence explicitly modeled or bounded, not a count of independent pairs.

**Success:** all planned comparisons are accounted for, findings are stable across source groups, and a predeclared recovery/specificity criterion is met or a defensible negative boundary replicates. **Kill:** evidence is driven by a few creators, labels cannot be adjudicated consistently, or missingness prevents complete-bank interpretation; restrict the population/claim and do not fill in outcomes.

**Precision:** even zero events in 100 iid source groups yields an approximately 2.95% one-sided 95% upper bound. About 299 independent zero-event units are required for a 1% bound, about 2,995 for .1%; correlated frames cannot supply those units. If a tighter claim is essential, use F5 before collecting outcomes.

**Data/cost:** Commons/V3C2 candidate groups, Open Images ordinary photos; 3–10 researcher-days for licensing/curation; laptop 12–36 h or rented 12–36 h (**USD 10.68–32.04**). Shared F1 artifacts must not be counted twice. **Unlocks:** external-population and complete-bank evidence; never legal ownership or universal specificity.

### F3. A separately trained autoencoder — strongly recommended for TIFS; required for broader transfer

**Hypothesis:** a mark optimized through the SD1.x encoder retains useful attribution through a regenerator with a separately trained encoder.

**Design:** SDXL is the first candidate because the inspected SDXL paper states its autoencoder was trained from scratch. Freeze model revision and independently establish encoder lineage from documentation/weights before running. A small new development cohort checks feasibility, then 300 untouched photographs with three seeds; use the existing SD1.5 channel as a paired reference. Strength .2 alone does not equate perceptual damage across architectures: predefine a quality-aware comparison using development data, retaining both nominal-strength and matched-quality panels. Do not retune F5. SD3/FLUX are optional later replications, not required to initiate an expensive sweep.

**Success:** source-majority recovery lower bound clears the prospectively chosen floor (the existing .85 is a candidate) while quality and false-binding criteria remain met. **Kill:** recovery collapses at comparable image fidelity, memory/runtime makes the scoped study infeasible, or the encoder is not independent; restrict the claim accordingly. A negative result can strengthen the boundary article without qualifying as successful transfer.

**Data/cost:** reuse the new preassigned confirmation cohort only under a jointly frozen protocol; laptop 12–48 h if memory fits, otherwise infeasible locally; rented 12–48 h (**USD 10.68–42.72**). 1–3 researcher-days. **Unlocks:** one independently trained encoder result, not all-autoencoder robustness.

### F4. Active re-marking and adaptive encoder-aware attack — required only for expanded security

**Hypothesis:** the passive attribution distinction survives a claimant who uses the public writer and, separately, detector/encoder information.

**Design:** keep two attack classes separate: (a) beta re-marks a regenerated alpha image before enrollment, with fixed public-writer access; (b) an encoder-aware, bounded-budget removal/false-binding adversary selected on development data. Evaluate on fresh source groups, with predeclared attempts, feedback access, quality/content constraints and both wrong-owner and abstention outcomes. Begin with a small new development screen after authorization; do not use current failures to optimize a held-out attack. A robustified-encoder route needs an existing vetted model or a separate training budget; training is not assumed in the estimate.

**Success:** a precise budgeted attack fails while correctness remains useful, with uncertainty stated. **Kill:** one reproducible admissible active forgery falsifies a universal claim; report attack rates and restrict the method to the passive setting. An attack that destroys image content is not counted as a meaningful success without the fixed admissibility rule.

**Data/cost:** 30 development and 300 future confirmation sources, no overlap; 2–5 researcher-days; laptop or rented 12–60 h (**USD 10.68–53.40**), excluding any new robust-encoder training. **Unlocks:** only the specified active access/budget, not cryptographic unforgeability. This plan does not reopen closed writer variants.

### F5. Larger independent cohorts and bank-size risk — conditional precision extension

**Hypothesis:** the operating point remains acceptable as independent source groups and distractor-bank size increase.

**Design:** target the risk claim first. A .1% zero-event upper bound requires roughly 3,000 independent groups before multiplicity/dependence allowances, not merely millions of pairs. Freeze query/reference separation and bank sizes, report per-query any-false-record outcomes, stratify owner multiplicity and valid fallback, and preserve q collision candidate counts separately from accepted attributions. Separate query and bank sampling where possible. Do not extrapolate uniform-code collision tables to actual q.

**Success:** the specified query-bank risk/utility holds across declared bank sizes with adequate independent precision. **Kill:** effective independent sample size is inadequate or query risk rises beyond the selected budget; narrow the scale claim. **Cost:** 1–3 weeks curation/engineering; laptop 60–240 h or rented 60–240 h (**USD 53.40–213.60**), highly sensitive to caching/bank size. **Unlocks:** bounded scaling/precision claims. Not necessary for an article that honestly retains percentage-scale uncertainty.

### F6. One stronger record verifier — conditional repair, not presumed cure

**Hypothesis:** one established learned copy descriptor or local-geometric verification stage improves look-alike rejection without sacrificing legitimate-regeneration recovery.

**Design:** select one option from V2 SSCD/DELG/soft-binding evidence on a separate development pool; compare it with frozen D1+B1 within the same pointer gate. Keep its metadata, model dependency, storage and inference costs visible. Freeze thresholds and the selection rule before new confirmation. Geometric verification is a hypothesis: the soft-binding benchmark does not establish universal improvement at matched FPR. Do not iterate new reference-free descriptor families or alter old E2 labels.

**Success:** prospectively defined gain in source-group specificity with no more than a pre-agreed recovery loss (candidate noninferiority margin 5 percentage points), followed by fresh confirmation. **Kill:** development trade-off fails or extra cost negates the intended scope; retain the negative result. **Cost:** 2–5 researcher-days; laptop or rented 8–24 h (**USD 7.12–21.36**), no retraining assumed. **Unlocks:** evidence for an improved registry verifier, not fulfillment of the original CLIP+pHash or lightweight-detector hypotheses.

### F7. Safety-checker and availability accounting — desk correction now, prospective sensitivity later

**Hypothesis:** policy-block and other unavailability patterns materially affect operational attribution estimates.

**Design:** first use retained statuses only (N3); this session already distinguishes 50 science-stage safety failures, three marking failures and dependent unavailable units (EV-67–69). A later prospective test logs safety flags and failure reasons before scoring, using lawful ordinary-content cohorts and the standard configured checker. If comparing checker configurations is scientifically justified, it requires an explicit later policy/protocol decision; do not bypass protections or regenerate blocked held-out images to replace missing results. Report operational success over all attempts and available-output success separately, without causal attribution from observational missingness alone.

**Success:** denominator effects and source/model clustering are documented; any causal claim comes only from an approved prospective design. **Kill:** missing outcomes cannot be recovered under the permitted design; keep uncertainty, not invented negatives. **Cost:** desk work 1–2 h, USD 0; future laptop or rented sensitivity panel 4–12 h (**USD 3.56–10.68**) plus 1–2 researcher-days. **Unlocks:** clearer availability interpretation; no retroactive pass.

A provisional **48–120 rental-hour envelope, USD 42.72–106.80 at the retrieved price**, is a planning allowance rather than the sum of overlapping rows or a measured forecast; allow roughly 1–3 researcher-weeks for curation, implementation compatibility and analysis. A later authorized development timing/fit check must justify any purchase. The present session spends **USD 0 on compute** and initiates none of this work.

If F1 supplies no informative comparative distinction and F2 shows only a narrow implementation-specific failure, do not pursue cosmetic method changes solely to reach Q1. Prepare a narrower empirical thesis chapter/case study and ask the supervisor at the appropriate review whether to change the research contribution. If an active attack succeeds, preserve it; do not redefine security after the fact. If access gaps prevent novelty checking, withhold priority claims regardless of positive experiments.
