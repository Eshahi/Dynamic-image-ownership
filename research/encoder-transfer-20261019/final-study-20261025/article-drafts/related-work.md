## Watermark insertion and the detection task

Watermark robustness depends on where information is introduced and what the detector receives. An image-domain residual, a modification of a diffusion model's initial noise, and a trained latent decoder traverse different computational paths. These distinctions matter for interpreting transfer: deploying a watermarking procedure on several generators does not establish that a fixed marked image survives reconstruction by another generator. F5, the case study here, modifies an existing image through optimization directed by the SD1.5 encoder. It remains an image-domain writer. Its use of encoder features does not make it an initial-noise watermark or validate latent insertion with a lightweight extractor. This classification follows the retained method account and constrains the comparisons below ([study synthesis](../01-literature-synthesis.md)).

Tree-Ring inserts a Fourier pattern into the initial Gaussian noise and detects it through diffusion inversion followed by comparison with the reference pattern [@wen2023treering]. Its experiments instantiate watermarking on Stable Diffusion and an ImageNet diffusion model; they do not freeze one writer and isolate survival through an independently trained autoencoder. Model access and inversion belong to the detection procedure, even when the original prompt and image are unavailable. Consequently, Tree-Ring's generation and detection results address a different observation model from an image-domain extractor operating on F5-marked inputs. [Primary method and experiments, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.20030v2); [evidence card](../literature/watermarks/01-tree-ring.md).

Decoder-based methods provide another distinction. Stable Signature fine-tunes the latent decoder to produce a fixed signature recovered by a pretrained image extractor; it leaves the diffusion process unchanged [@fernandez2023signature]. LaWa instead trains message-conditioned residual modules at intermediate decoder stages while freezing the base decoder [@rezaei2024lawa]. Both therefore incorporate watermarking into the latent-to-image mapping, but neither implements F5's image-space optimization through a fixed encoder. Their detection resources and learned readouts also differ. A common association with latent diffusion is insufficient to treat these insertion sites as one robustness class. [Stable Signature §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.15435v2), [LaWa §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.05868v3); [Stable Signature card](../literature/watermarks/02-stable-signature.md), [LaWa card](../literature/watermarks/08-lawa.md).

Among post-hoc learned methods, Watermark Anything with Localized Messages (WAM) predicts an additive image signal and extracts a watermark mask together with message estimates at pixel level [@T3WAM2411]. Native image presence, localized recovery, and agreement with a supplied candidate message are different endpoints. Its neural-purification experiments report bit accuracy under several reconstruction severities, rather than the source-majority semantic/content-binding endpoint used for F5. Our WAM comparison uses a pinned released checkpoint whose training population differs from the paper's main model; the published operating-point performance cannot simply be transferred to that release. [WAM §§3–5 and Appendix G.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.07231v2); [implementation and evidence card](../../design-research/T3-baseline-fidelity/cards/WAM.md).

This endpoint distinction extends across the study. External known-message presence asks whether enough recovered bits agree with the supplied message. F5's C endpoint additionally requires its semantic decoding and content-consistency conditions. Presence is therefore not semantic binding, and semantic binding is not proof of legal ownership or adaptive forgery resistance. The comparison arms provide context for removal resistance under stated information and quality budgets; their counts do not form an equal-payload, equal-quality, empirically equal-FPR ranking ([reviewed development results](../../results-2b.md); [positioning boundary](../02-positioning.md)).

## Generative reconstruction as an attack

Zhao et al. formulate regeneration as embedding, Gaussian perturbation, and reconstruction, and evaluate VAE and diffusion instances against several post-hoc watermarks [@zhao2024invisible]. Their detector-independent tradeoff depends on the watermark's image-space bound, local embedding gain, and noise standard deviation; a separate reconstruction condition supports the utility argument. These premises prevent interpreting the result as unconditional removal of every imperceptible watermark. The paper's experiments and its Tree-Ring defense appendix also caution against assigning one universal fate to every insertion site. The theory motivates examining signal after encoding and perturbation, but supplies no measured gain or removal threshold for F5. [§§3–5, Theorems 4.3–4.4 and Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01953v3); [evidence card](../literature/attacks/zhao-provable.md).

WAVES broadens evaluation to single reconstruction, repeated diffusion rinsing, and informed attacks, while reporting detector degradation alongside image quality [@an2024waves]. It reports Tree-Ring degradation under regeneration and rinsing in settings that differ from Zhao et al.'s defense evaluation. Different attack recipes, populations, and operating points make those findings protocol-dependent rather than an isolated contradiction about a single channel. Repeated low-strength regeneration is thus established prior methodology. Our two-pass rinse is a bounded stress test, and multiplying single-pass retention probabilities would require an independence assumption unavailable when each pass consumes the previous output. [WAVES §§3–4 and Figure 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08573v3); [evidence card](../literature/attacks/waves.md).

VINE combines blur-like surrogate corruptions with a generative-prior watermark encoder and a learned message decoder; W-Bench evaluates stochastic and deterministic regeneration alongside other generative edits [@lu2025vine]. Its reported regeneration resilience makes VINE-R a useful comparison arm, but not an explanation of F5's behavior. Fidelity, payload, thresholds, and attack settings remain specific to each protocol. Moreover, the inspected paper describes SDXL-Turbo, whereas the pinned released constructor initializes SD-Turbo; we preserve that discrepancy instead of treating the paper's backbone name as checkpoint provenance. [VINE §§3–4 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18775v2); [paper card](../literature/attacks/vine-wbench.md), [released implementation card](../../design-research/T3-baseline-fidelity/cards/VINE.md).

MarkNull is particularly close prior work on proxy-latent manipulation [@cao2026marknull]. It uses a public diffusion proxy, inversion, and a latent alignment objective with fidelity penalties without querying the target detector. Its experiments include cross-backbone attacks. However, its transfer argument assumes compatible latent coordinates and the same discrete inversion scheme, together with controlled vector-field discrepancy; the post-hoc argument adds an off-manifold residual premise. Empirical cross-backbone success does not prove those premises for arbitrary independently trained encoders. Nor does angular orthogonality alone establish statistical independence. MarkNull therefore motivates a related attack family without supplying a theorem for F5's SDXL removal. [§§5–7 and Appendix E.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10166v1); [evidence card](../literature/attacks/marknull.md).

## Autoencoder provenance and the limits of reconstruction evidence

A change in diffusion-model name need not introduce a newly trained encoder. SDXL's technical report explicitly distinguishes its from-scratch autoencoder from SD2.x's SD1.x-derived autoencoder with a fine-tuned decoder [@podell2023sdxl]. It also evaluates reconstruction quality separately from generation. This makes lineage relevant to transfer testing, while avoiding a stronger interpretation: independent weight training establishes neither disjoint training images nor statistical independence of local mappings. Full SD1.5 and SDXL img2img pipelines also differ beyond their autoencoders. [SDXL §2.4 and Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.01952v1); [provenance card](../literature/encoders/podell-sdxl.md).

Prior autoencoder attacks already demonstrate that retention is method-dependent. Stable Signature reports that reconstruction with the generation autoencoder removes its signature more effectively than several foreign codecs. LaWa reports substantial bit retention under its corresponding KL-f8 codec, contrasting with Stable Signature under that comparison. These are reconstruction results with different writing procedures, and even their PSNR reference images require care; they do not establish a universal same-encoder advantage. Our distinction between foreign-autoencoder round trips and full noising–denoising reconstruction builds on this literature rather than treating autoencoder attacks as new [@fernandez2023signature; @rezaei2024lawa]. [Stable Signature §7.1/Figure 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.15435v2), [LaWa §4.7/Figure 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.05868v3).

Behera's rank-ratio study is close prior work on direction scrambling and VAE-specific co-design [@behera2026rankratio]. The inspected author manuscript studies deterministic round trips, trains a watermark through a frozen VAE, and explicitly reports that SD1.5 co-design does not retain its performance under SDXL reconstruction. This overlap limits any novelty claim based only on independent-encoder failure. Its isotropic local-linear rank argument does not furnish a worst-case readable-response bound for an optimized F5 residual. The article's IEEE Access identity and DOI are resolved through official metadata, but the inspected author manuscript retains provisional header information and its identity with the inaccessible final publisher text remains unverified. [Inspected author text, §V/Table 13 and co-design limitation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/410879983_A_Rank-Ratio_Bound_for_Pixel-Space_Signal_Fragility_in_Latent-Diffusion_Autoencoders); [version-qualified card](../literature/transfer-theory/rank-ratio-unverified.md).

PhaseMark directly modifies Fourier phases in encoded VAE latents, enforces Hermitian symmetry, and decodes; detection re-encodes and checks payload agreement [@lee2026phasemark]. Its reported VAE-B/VAE-C attacks reference learned compression codecs. They are not evidence of transfer through SDXL or FLUX diffusion autoencoders, and its diffusion-regeneration condition does not establish a named independent-backbone grid. The later Lee–Cho taxonomy also leaves its proposed semantic carrier untested [@lee2026taxonomy]. We cite that inspected restatement on its own terms; the original Semantic Imprinting Hypothesis remains identity-verified only and is not evidence for unread methods. Survival under editing does not identify a carrier. [PhaseMark §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13128v1), [taxonomy §§5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09755v1); [access and comparison card](../literature/watermarks/13-phase-mark-and-imprinting.md).

The present contribution is consequently bounded. Existing development observations distinguish successful foreign-autoencoder round trips from failed full regeneration for one encoder-directed image writer; they do not isolate an encoder cause or validate a transport mechanism. Predicting later detection from signed, detector-specific transport is a prospective proposal requiring new measurements and frozen forecasts, not a measured contribution of these results. This positioning makes no exhaustive-search or priority claim ([development results](../../results-2b.md); [prospective positioning](../02-positioning.md)).
