# Esser et al. 2024 — SD3 rectified flow (V2)

- **Exact source:** Patrick Esser, Sumith Kulal, Andreas Blattmann, Rahim Entezari, Jonas Müller, Harry Saini, Yam Levi, Dominik Lorenz, Axel Sauer, Frederic Boesel, Dustin Podell, Tim Dockhorn, Zion English, Kyle Lacey, Alex Goodwin, Yannik Marek, Robin Rombach, *Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers for High-Resolution Image Synthesis*, arXiv:2403.03206v1, 2024. https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03206v1. Accessed 2026-10-25 UTC.
- **Method inspected:** §3 (rectified-flow paths), §4 (multimodal architecture), §5.2.1 and Appendix B.3. The image AE has f=8 and selected latent channels d=16; latents are normalized by empirical mean/std. This differs from SD1.x/SDXL's f8 four-channel representation. The sampling trajectory is rectified flow, so an SD1.5/DDIM `strength` is not the same physical noise level.
- **Experiment inspected:** §5.1 tests flow objectives and timestep distributions. §5.2.1/Table 3 tests 4, 8 and 16 channel AEs at f8; reported PSNR 25.12, 26.40, 28.62 and SSIM .75, .79, .86. This is an AE reconstruction ablation, not encoder transfer.
- **Direct evidence:** SD3 research model uses a 16-channel f8 AE, empirically different architecture and latent normalization. The paper does not explicitly document whether the final released SD3/3.5 AE checkpoint was randomly initialized or adapted from an earlier checkpoint.
- **Inference for this study:** Strong architecture-level separation from SD1.5, but **weight-training independence remains unverified** until checkpoint provenance/source is inspected. Different denoiser and flow schedule are confounders.
- **Limits:** Do not treat the 2024 research paper as proof of SD3.5 Medium's exact AE weights, license or access; those require its specific model card/config. Neither paper nor Table 3 measures watermark transfer.
