# T1: regeneration attack standards and frozen-design recommendations

Date: 2026-10-19. This is pre-outcome research for the independent-encoder development study. No attack, detector or image-scoring run was performed. The recommendation is to retain the fixed primary SDXL .2 cell and add **one** low-cost secondary: two sequential SDXL .1 passes. This follows the established rinsing attack family while preserving the study's encoder question. It is an SDXL adaptation, not a reproduction of WAVES. Caption-guided regeneration is the second-ranked option, conditional on a pre-outcome caption policy and remaining whole-study budget; do not add both automatically.

## Design implications

1. **The primary is mechanism isolation, not a benchmark replica.** DDIM20/no CFG/empty text and SDXL1024 preserve the fixed question. W-Bench's 60–240 refers to training-noise time, with 3–12 denoiser calls in the inspected implementation. Do not label strength .2 “200 noise steps” without reading the actual scheduler sequence. Record schedule length, executed timesteps, alpha/sigma or log-SNR, VAE posterior mode/sampling, eta, guidance, resolution and all resize operations. T5 should freeze the actual numerical mapping.
2. **Separate representations from generators.** SD2.1 vs SD1.4 benchmarks do not establish independent encoder ancestry. The SDXL-only VAE diagnostic and resize-only diagnostic distinguish compression/resampling from diffusion. Official SDXL VAE remains primary; adaptive/robustified encoders belong in proposals.
3. **Do not overstate removal or identity.** Report common detector presence, native operating point, payload/owner decoding and registry acceptance separately. BA near .5 does not by itself certify low false positives. PSNR or CLIP resemblance does not establish same-instance identity. Preserve per-source results and missing outputs; use source, not seed or pair count, as the independent unit.
4. **Report performance against quality, not strength alone.** Show paired PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS for all attempted outputs, fixed-cell detection with uncertainty, and descriptive quality-survival curves. No post-result quality filtering or selecting the most favorable attack. The tiny study cannot import WAVES dataset-dependent normalized-Q cutoffs or stable FID estimates without qualification.

## Secondary choices and marginal cost

**Rank 1: SDXL rinse2, .1 then .1.** Same four methods and source set; three fixed seed pairs (12,112), (13,113), (14,114). Reuse the corresponding R1 .1 first-pass output. Pass two uses the same DDIM20/eta0/empty/no-CFG policy. To reuse the actual R1 output without hidden change, start pass two from the saved 512 image, upscale by the frozen Lanczos policy, regenerate1024, downsample512. This introduces an extra resize roundtrip; name it explicitly and save the intermediate. A resize-only twice-applied control is required. Do not claim equal quality or equal effective noise to one .2 pass. The comparison addresses repeated low-strength removal, not calibrated equivalence. No detector-dependent stopping. The source-majority seed labels remain12/13/14; record both pass seeds separately and pair them identically across arms. There is no universal standard requiring the same noise on both passes. The inspected WAVES helper reinitializes a generator to1024, but draws attack Gaussian noise from the global torch RNG without that generator, so it does not prescribe identical noise between passes. Deterministically disjoint pass seeds make the repeated corruption reproducible without intentionally reusing the same noise realization.

For four marked arms, marginal second-pass calls =12n=720–1,440 at n60–120. At an **assumed planning rate**, not measured performance, of3/6/10 seconds per 1024 .1 call, this is0.60–1.20 /1.20–2.40 /2.00–4.00 GPU hours. Adding a separately regenerated unmarked C0 arm gives15n=900–1,800 calls, or0.75–1.50 /1.50–3.00 /2.50–5.00 hours. Add scoring/I/O overhead in the integrated budget. Wrong-key calls need detection, not new generation. Require the parent to count the C0 policy explicitly.

**Rank 2, conditional: caption-conditioned SDXL .2.** A single descriptive source caption, identical across all four methods/C0, frozen before outputs. Prefer existing licensed source caption metadata; otherwise a pinned free captioner applied once to the unmarked development image under a fixed prompt. This adds a model, memory and caption-preparation cost. Do not generate captions from each watermarked result, optimize wording or invoke a paid API. Keep CFG1 if isolating text conditioning; a CFG>1 setting changes both prompt and guidance and must be named as such. Original-generation-prompt WAVES is a stronger-information setting than inferred captions. One variant adds12n marked calls (15n with C0); at6/12seconds per .2 call,1.2–2.4/2.4–4.8h marked, or1.5–3/3–6h including C0. These are scenario bounds, not server benchmarks.

**Selection:** freeze at most rinse2 within the base study. Adopt only if the integrated conservative reservation, including all core channels, embedding, download/setup, transfer and margin, is ≤15h and≤$15. At $r/hour, marginal compute cost is r times the above hours plus storage/transfer. Do not assert affordability by allocating the whole budget to T1. If n120 or FLUX consumes the reservation, omit the optional rinse channel before freezing and record the reason. Caption regeneration stays an explicitly costed reserve unless T2/T5 demonstrate room. No automatic outcome-dependent switch.

Do not execute CtrlRegen training, UnMarker, RAVEN, MarkNull, WAW, DRIFT, SemanticRegen or pixel-space DiffPure in core. Each changes attacker access, training, compute or same-content assumptions and would dilute the cheapest decisive question. Costed adaptive-encoder and pixel-space proposals are reasonable follow-ups; a negative generic-transfer result needs no increasingly strong attack to establish failure.

## Coverage and unresolved gaps

Covered every named family: W-Bench/VINE, WAVES, Zhao, CtrlRegen/+, UnMarker, multipass rinsing, original DiffPure, MarkNull, RAVEN and WAW. Recent 2025–2026 sweep additionally inspected the competition winner, SemanticRegen, Ni (quarantined), and September2026 DRIFT. Search log states exclusions and inaccessible routes. The standard space includes stochastic reconstruction, deterministic inversion, repeated reconstruction, caption control, pure-noise semantic/spatial control, learned removal, spectral optimization and re-watermarking. These are considered, not all executed.

Remaining scientific/implementation gaps: final-version RAVEN comparison; exact RAVEN checkpoint; CtrlRegen sampler/resolution; version-pinned attack settings and complete prompt policies for several papers; latest trained-attack release licenses/weights; actual server timing; image-caption availability. These gaps limit benchmark-reproduction claims but do not block the fixed independent-encoder question. Latest paper claims are not accepted solely because V2 was reached. No complete novelty or universal-robustness claim follows from this search.
