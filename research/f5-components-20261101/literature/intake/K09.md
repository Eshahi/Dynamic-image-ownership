## Full paper cards

### K09-J01 — Safe testing

**Citation/class:** Peter Grünwald, Rianne de Heide, Wouter M. Koolen. “Safe testing.” JRSS B 86(5), 1091–1128, 2024; online 2024-03-07. DOI [10.1093/jrsssb/qkae011](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssb/qkae011). Canonical [publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/86/5/1091/7623686). **Q1J**, SJR evidence above; old preprint does not change final publication year.

**Fulltext inspected:** cached pp.3,5–6,21,27: Proposition 1, conditional e-variable definition, Proposition 2/test martingales, stopping-time discussion, Gaussian-location power example.

**Source finding:** An e-variable has null expectation at most one, giving a level-alpha test at 1/alpha. Sequential multiplication requires the next factor to have conditional null expectation at most one given past information; arbitrary marginal e-values are insufficient. Proposition 2 supports optional continuation under this condition. The Gaussian-location example on p.27 reports approximately 2.2-fold sample cost at alpha=.05 and 1.7 at .01 for its setting, not watermark data.

**Threat/quality/cost:** Statistical nulls and sequential evidence, not an image attacker. Independent-encoder regeneration: not tested. T4/T5: not tested. Human visibility/image quality: inapplicable. Side information is a specified null family, filtration and chosen betting construction; evidence evaluation and sample requirements depend on that model.

**PROPOSED transfer/failure:** Use this paper to state exactly what would license multiplying tier evidence. No such conditioning proof currently follows from F5's 320 chips. A second score computed from the same image is not a new independent experiment merely because its carrier differs. The sample-cost numbers must not become projected F5 costs.

### K09-J02 — The only admissible way of merging arbitrary e-values

**Citation/class:** Ruodu Wang. “The only admissible way of merging arbitrary e-values.” Biometrika 112(2), asaf020, 2025; online 2025-03-17. DOI [10.1093/biomet/asaf020](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asaf020); [canonical record](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article/112/2/asaf020/8086785). **Q1J**.

**Fulltext inspected:** author PDF pp.2–4,8–9, especially Theorem 1, Lemma 4 and section 5 subclasses. Publisher's Theorem 1 also retrieved.

**Source finding:** For increasing Borel mergers valid for arbitrary e-values with unrestricted dependence, every merger is dominated by a weighted arithmetic average including the constant 1; these averages characterize admissibility (Theorem 1). Section 5 permits other constructions for restricted subclasses, e.g. known cross-moment bounds. It does not say arithmetic averaging is optimal for every known joint distribution. This is a proof paper with no image-performance table.

**Threat/quality/cost:** Null expectation over arbitrary dependence; independent regeneration/T4/T5/human visibility: none. Side information: individually valid e-values and fixed mixture weights; O(number of tiers) merger arithmetic, excluding extraction.

**PROPOSED transfer/failure:** A fixed e-mixture is a defensible option if F5 tier e-values can first be justified. Neither a learned classifier score nor 1/p is automatically an e-value. Same-image selection of the largest e-value, or weights chosen from those values, is not the fixed mixture the theorem validates. Restricting the null class could improve power but needs evidence separate from the observed 60 positives.

### K09-J03 — Testing the mean and variance by e-processes

**Citation/class:** Yixuan Fan, Zhanyi Jiao, Ruodu Wang. “Testing the mean and variance by e-processes.” Biometrika 112(1), asae049, 2025; first published 2024-09-30. DOI [10.1093/biomet/asae049](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asae049); [canonical record](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article/112/1/asae049/7796539). **Q1J**.

**Fulltext inspected:** author pp.4,8,11,13,16: section 2.1 conditional hypotheses; Theorem 1; section 4.1 equation (5); section 4.2 combining conditions; section 5.1 simulations.

**Source finding:** Tests require conditional mean and variance bounds, allowing nonstationarity and dependence. Theorem 1 yields E=(X−mu)_+²/sigma² and P=1/(1+E). Equation (5) multiplies 1−lambda_i+lambda_i E_i with predictable lambda_i. Shape assumptions can sharpen bounds. Section 5.1 alternates Gaussian/Laplace observations and estimates rejection rates over 1,000 repetitions at .05. Section 4.2's p-combinations rely on conditional validity, not arbitrary marginal dependence.

**Threat/quality/cost:** Statistical moment-null study, no watermark, independently trained regeneration, T4/T5 or visibility test. Side information: genuine conditional moment bounds; optional symmetry/unimodality must also be justified. Per-observation updates are cheap; obtaining defensible bounds is the hard requirement.

**PROPOSED transfer/failure:** A conservative moment-based reader is a possible fallback if Gaussian tails are unjustified. Estimating variance on a small development null pool does not establish a uniform conditional variance bound. Public adaptive images and content-dependent carrier selection can break the required conditional hypothesis before any combination is attempted.

### K09-J04 — Aggregating dependent signals with heavy-tailed combination tests

**Citation/class:** Lin Gui, Yuchao Jiang, Jingshu Wang. “Aggregating dependent signals with heavy-tailed combination tests.” Biometrika 112(4), asaf038, 2025; online 2025-05-30. DOI [10.1093/biomet/asaf038](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asaf038); [canonical record](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article/112/4/asaf038/8153900). **Q1J**.

**Fulltext inspected:** author pp.9,11–12,17,24; Theorems 2.3/2.6; sections 3.1/4; discussion. Final open PMC text was also inspected.

**Source finding:** Under its pairwise-normal and tail conditions, heavy-tailed combinations have small-alpha asymptotic validity; Theorem 2.6 gives asymptotic equivalence to weighted Bonferroni. Section 3.1 uses correlations 0,.5,.99, n=5 or100, alpha=.05 or .0005 and one million repetitions. Section 4 discusses tail dependence, including t statistics; the discussion warns that arbitrary dependence and finite alpha can inflate type-I error. No universal exact FPR guarantee for correlated watermark scores follows.

**Threat/quality/cost:** Dependent statistical signals; no image threats, independent regeneration, T4/T5 or human assessment. Requires valid marginal p-values and stated joint/tail conditions; transformation/summation is inexpensive.

**PROPOSED transfer/failure:** Do not select Cauchy/harmonic-mean fusion merely because it is advertised as dependence-robust. F5's finite 320-chip statistic and adaptive content do not imply pairwise Gaussian extreme-tail behavior. This paper is negative evidence against asserting a free power improvement over a union-bound reader.

### K09-J05 — Confidence on the focal

**Citation/class:** Ying Jin, Zhimei Ren. “Confidence on the focal: conformal prediction with selection-conditional coverage.” JRSS B 87(4),1239–1259,2025; online 2025-04-15. DOI [10.1093/jrsssb/qkaf016](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssb/qkaf016); [canonical record](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/87/4/1239/8113856). **Q1J**.

## Proposed section C — candidate changes and decision conditions

Everything in this section is **PROPOSED**, not a ruling, implementation or experiment authorization.

### C.1 Establish the null before reducing a threshold

K09 currently uses normalized 320-chip scores, with unconstrained segmented decoding over 32 binary decisions (2^32 possible words), decoded threshold8.259326826136963 and recomputed threshold4.982033056390042 at the recorded roster size1. These values and the decoded-OR-recomputed endpoint come from the existing K09 analysis; they were not retuned here.

The required object is a conditional statement, for every permitted null/attack distribution:
P(score >= t | information used to choose q_now, carrier, tier, scale, codebook and key policy) <= alpha.
If a random-sign argument conditions on a fixed image/projection but the chosen code or projection depends on those signs, the fixed-vector premise may no longer hold. A public fixed seed also does not supply fresh randomization against a key-aware attacker. J01/J03/J05 and T03 make these distinctions explicit in different settings.

Write separate nulls for unmarked natural images, wrong owners, copied marks and semantically colliding content. Validity for one is not validity for all. Do not convert 0/1254 development null calls into a 1e-6 empirical guarantee; dependence between calls also prevents treating every call as a new independent image. An iid zero-failure bound would itself require iid sampling and a declared confidence level.

### C.2 Constrained decoding is a promising algorithmic change with a conditional caveat

Let a fixed reference word q0 have32 bits; restrict decoding to its Hamming ball of radius r. Let a_j(0),a_j(1) be the two segment contributions to the unnormalized numerator, S(q0)=sum_j a_j(q0_j), and Delta_j=a_j(1−q0_j)−a_j(q0_j). With the existing denominator fixed for all words, the constrained maximum is [S(q0) + sum of the largest at most r positive Delta_j]/||p||. Sorting32 gains suffices; exhaustive 2^32 search is unnecessary. This is a derivation for the stated separable score, not a performance result.

The candidate count becomes V(32,r)=sum from k=0 to r of binomial(32,k). A union bound can replace2^32 by V only if each candidate's tail bound remains valid after conditioning on the rule that constructs the ball. Choosing q0 from the same suspect to maximize agreement and then pretending it was fixed is invalid. Candidate overlap does not invalidate the union bound; it makes an independence formula inappropriate. A soft-content shortlist with unknown null selection must retain the full-search bound or obtain a selection-aware calibration/proof.

Even a correct smaller multiplicity does not add semantic information: the same nearby hash can arise from two different images. Do not call recovered bits an ownership/content match until the separate T4/T5 predicate is satisfied.

### C.3 Fuse at an explicit family-wide false-positive budget

Define the complete family first: tiers, decoded/recomputed branches, scales, candidate owners and any shortlisted words. A safe baseline is to allocate alpha across that family and reject on the union of branch rejections. This remains valid under arbitrary dependence if individual branch bounds are valid. If two internal branches each claim alpha and are ORed, the generic bound is2alpha; adding a second such tier can yield4alpha. These are upper bounds, not measured FPR inflation, and nesting or a proved joint test may sharpen them.

A fixed weighted e-average is another proposal (J02), conditional on valid input e-values. Multiplying tier evidence needs conditional validity (J01/J03), not visual carrier diversity or small Pearson correlation. A learned fusion function would need its own null calibration after the function and all selection rules are frozen; held-out pixels remain prohibited in this task. J04 supplies no automatic justification for unadjusted Cauchy fusion. FDR across a batch is not the per-suspect/owner false-positive guarantee needed here.

### C.4 Treat complementary signal as evidence to diagnose, not a new content endpoint

Gate1 N1 found complementary *detection*: SDXL .1 robust7/60 versus second-tier52/60 (second-tier content32/60); SDXL .2 robust0 versus second30 (content12); .4 second5 (content3). K3 robust23, second16, either29; frozen C5 versus an exploratory composite16/60. These are ruling-provided development observations, not new runs. The frozen C endpoint must stay frozen. T4/T5 for the composite remain unmeasured.

The useful reader output is a structured evidence vector: tier signal presence, decoded payload, agreement with current content, and any separately justified content-verification result. “Either tier positive” is only signal presence. WAM demonstrates how a detector can correctly identify a pasted watermark without proving the surrounding content belongs to that owner. Gaussian Shading supplies an explicit semantic-forgery caution.

The second tier is worth future investigation only if it adds content-specific evidence after a shared family-wise null budget, with no T4/T5 degradation. Changing detector thresholds to turn 52 detections into 52 content matches would change the claim rather than solve the problem.

### C.5 Resolve the scale confound before attributing architecture transfer

Gate1 N2 pairs SD1.5 at512 with SDXL/K3 at1024 and Lanczos resizing. Therefore model family and scale are confounded. The literature here gives no basis to attribute the entire loss to independently trained encoders. A future design should compare saved development observations under a fixed resolution/canonicalization policy and report scale-specific null behavior, without selecting the best scale per suspect unless its multiplicity is included.

**Proposed disposition:** KEEP the existing reader as the labelled comparator. MODIFY the statistical specification first; develop a constrained-decoding candidate only under a stated conditional null; CONSIDER complementary tiers with family-wide error control and a separate content predicate. REJECT claims that e-values, an empirical null fit, a nearby content hash or OR fusion automatically solve T3/T4/T5. This is a proposal to the judge, not a passed gate.
