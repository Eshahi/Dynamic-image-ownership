## 4. Inspected paper cards

### K07-J1 — Attack-aware strength and the cost of a second layer

**Citation:** Yichao Tang, Chuntao Wang, Shijun Xiang, Yiu-Ming Cheung. “A Robust Reversible Watermarking Scheme Using Attack-Simulation-Based Adaptive Normalization and Embedding.” *IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security* 19:4114–4129, 2024. DOI:10.1109/TIFS.2024.3372811. Proposed class **Q1J**; provenance and quartile above.

SOURCE: §§III–IV select stable PHT moment carriers, normalize according to simulated attack sensitivity, and increase STDM quantization levels until the candidate's bits survive selected attacks. A reversible auxiliary layer stores recovery information. Detector side information includes secret carrier/key/configuration; the clean original is not required for ordinary mark extraction. Simulation uses AWGN, JPEG2000, and mean filtering; evaluation also covers other signal-processing/geometric changes. Separate 1,000-image BOWS2 sets are used for stability estimation and evaluation.

SOURCE NUMBERS: §IV-D, pp.4124–4125/Table IX discussion reports mean PSNR **40.25 dB after robust embedding versus 39.09 dB after auxiliary embedding**. §IV-C3, p.4123/Table VI discussion reports auxiliary storage **23,027.3→2,531.2 bits**, an 89% reduction; this is storage, not a measured 89% distortion saving. §IV-C4 and §IV-E discuss attack-specific compromises; JPEG is weaker partly because it was omitted from the simulated attack set. The conclusion explicitly does not guarantee unknown-attack robustness.

**Threat coverage:** no diffusion regeneration, independently trained encoder transfer, T4 copy-paste decision, or T5 semantic binding experiment. SSIM/LPIPS all-image gate and controlled human visibility evidence are MISSING.

**K07 transfer, JUDGMENT:** the paper supplies a concrete allocation principle—pay for tested carrier/bit stability—and direct evidence that a subsequent layer changes total quality. Port the measurement question, not PHT/reversible architecture or conventional-attack robustness. Its attacker distribution is much simpler than F5's nonlinear foreign regeneration. A second layer can have different functions in the two systems; its measured PSNR cost does not establish that F5's second tier is removable.

### K07-T1 — ROAR as a reader-side quality control

**Citation:** Hanyi Wang, Han Fang, Shi-Lin Wang, Ee-Chien Chang. “ROAR: Reducing Inversion Error in Generative Image Watermarking.” *ICCV* 2025:19742–19751. DOI:10.1109/ICCV51701.2025.01836. Proposed class **TOP**, with full supplement inspected.

SOURCE: §§4–5 restore distorted images through an entropy-adaptive mixture of experts, then optimize an inverted latent to reconstruct the restored image under unconditional diffusion. Reader access includes generator/VAE, restoration network, and watermark mechanism/key; no original prompt is needed. Default optimization is **20 iterations**, with **50 inversion steps** (p.19749/Fig7/Table5).

SOURCE NUMBERS: Table1 reports GS mean conventional-distortion TPR **.912/.924→1.000/1.000** for the two generator settings, with unchanged FID **25.20±.22** because the marked outputs are unchanged. Table2's reconstruction variants attain TPR 1 at attack PSNRs spanning **32.09 to 25.64**; purification at strength .5/.7 gives TPR **.980/.663**, with attack PSNR **18.75/16.65**. These are attacked-output qualities, not the writer's clean fidelity gate. §5.4 text labels a diffusion reconstruction SD-v2.1 while Table2 labels SD-v1.4; this inconsistency is unresolved.

**Threat coverage:** reconstruction/purification tested; independent encoder training lineage is not established by model labels. No T4/T5 acceptance or content-binding experiment. Clean all-image PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS and controlled human invisibility verdict are MISSING.

**K07 transfer, JUDGMENT:** improved recovery need not consume more embedding distortion. This is a useful comparison against indiscriminate writer strengthening. Literal ROAR is a poor F5 replacement: F5 does not use DDIM inversion, and the reader would acquire a trained network plus repeated diffusion optimization. Unchanged FID in a generated-image protocol does not prove F5's per-image quality constraints. Reserve it as a mechanistic control, not a proposed extra UNet or new training campaign.

### K07-T2 — VINE's explicit robustness/quality trade-off and scale confound

**Citation:** Shilin Lu, Zihan Zhou, Jiayou Lu, Yuanzhi Zhu, Adams Wai-Kin Kong. “Robust Watermarking Using Generative Priors Against Image Editing: From Benchmarking to Advances.” *ICLR* 2025. Official publication/PDF linked above; no DOI required. Proposed class **TOP**.

SOURCE: §§3.1–3.3 use blurring as a surrogate for editing's frequency response, an adapted SDXL-Turbo encoder with skip connections, and ConvNeXt-B 100-bit extraction. Loss combines message BCE with YUV MSE, LPIPS and adversarial terms. VINE-R additionally trains through Instruct-Pix2Pix using a straight-through estimator. Detector receives suspect image and reference message, not the original image.

SOURCE NUMBERS: Table1 p.8, **means over 10,000 pairs**, gives VINE-B **40.51/.9954/.0029** PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS and VINE-R **37.34/.9934/.0063**. Averaged stochastic/deterministic regeneration TPR@.1%FPR is **91.03/99.25%** versus **99.66/99.98%**. Table2 p.10, blur A→B increases stochastic TPR **54.61→86.85%** while PSNR falls **38.21→35.85 dB**. Appendix D's residual resizing changes the physical scale of a fixed blur kernel; training is 256 and evaluation 512. Appendix F specifies full training and 25-step Pix2Pix fine-tuning.

**Threat coverage:** §4.1 tests stochastic regeneration timesteps60–240 and deterministic inversion 15/25/35/45 steps, plus global/local editors. A controlled matrix of independently trained encoder families is MISSING. Local inpainting resilience is not T4 transplant rejection; semantic binding/T5 is MISSING. Means do not establish every-image quality. Controlled human assessment is MISSING.

**K07 transfer, JUDGMENT:** allocation must account for the channel and resolution, not just texture. Stronger survival can consume quality; a richer writer can also alter that trade-off, so comparisons must match the final quality constraints. No low-frequency universal-survival theorem follows from the observed spectra. Do not import a trained generative writer as a cheap budget adjustment or infer F5 gains from VINE's averages.

### K07-T3 — WAVES separates attack quality from detection success

**Citation:** Bang An, Mucong Ding, Tahseen Rabbani, Aakriti Agrawal, Yuancheng Xu, Chenghao Deng, Sicheng Zhu, Abdirisak Mohamed, Yuxin Wen, Tom Goldstein, Furong Huang. “WAVES: Benchmarking the Robustness of Image Watermarks.” *ICML*, PMLR235:1456–1492, 2024. Official record linked above. Proposed class **TOP**.

SOURCE: §§3–4 measure detection at fixed .1% FPR against attack-quality curves. Eight quality metrics are normalized using 10th/90th percentiles of the benchmark's attacked-image distribution; Appendix E.4 averages within four metric categories, then across categories. Three datasets each supply 5,000 reference images. Detectors retain their method-specific keys, messages and, where applicable, generative inversion models.

SOURCE NUMBERS: Table3 p.8, Regen-Diff average TPR is **.612 Tree-Ring, .001 Stable Signature, .943 StegaStamp**; normalized attack degradation **.323/.300/.327** respectively. Appendix F.2 uses victim SD2.1 and regeneration SD1.4, timesteps40–200, plus compression autoencoders/KL bottlenecks and repeated regeneration. These averages mix strengths; they are not matched strict-clean-quality F5 results.

**Threat coverage:** substantive T3 reconstruction/regeneration benchmarking, but SD-version labels alone do not establish independent encoder training. SDXL-VAE appears in an adversarial embedding attack, not a controlled independent-encoder regeneration matrix. Neither T4 transplant rejection nor T5 semantic collision is established by user-ID or erasure tests. Human F5 invisibility remains MISSING.

**K07 transfer, JUDGMENT:** use separate axes for clean embedding quality, attacked-image content preservation, and detection/valid binding. Benchmark-normalized averages cannot replace F5's three conjunctive per-image thresholds: changing the attack collection changes the normalization, and averaging can conceal a failing metric. The benchmark also cautions against judging a budget by bit accuracy alone. No out-of-scope attack experiments are proposed here.

### K07-P1 — Pixel Seal's native-resolution JND and metric blind spots

**Citation:** Tom?? Sou?ek, Pierre Fernandez, Hady Elsahar, Sylvestre-Alvise Rebuffi, Valeriu Lacatusu, Tuan Tran, Tom Sander, Alexandre Mourachko. “Pixel Seal: Adversarial-only training for invisible image and video watermarking.” arXiv:2512.16874, 2025, v1. Proposed class **PRE**, zero floor credit.

SOURCE: §§4.1–4.3 replace perceptual training losses with adversarial/message losses, use staged strength reduction, and multiply the upsampled residual by a JND map computed at original resolution. The mechanism explicitly addresses spill from edges into flat regions. Decoder uses the suspect and message; training includes a discriminator, not a diffusion regenerator.

SOURCE NUMBERS: Table2 p.11 reports mean PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS **48.9/.9905/.0013** on 1,000 generated images. Table3 p.12, 100-photo ablation, removes JND: PSNR **47.0→43.1**, predicted JND **.44→1.34**, combined conventional-attack bit accuracy **.94→.97**. Fixed 256-resolution training instead of the full-resolution recipe lowers combined bit accuracy **.94→.68**. Appendix p.18 lists valuemetric, geometric and compression attacks, with no diffusion regeneration.

**Human/metric distinction:** §5.1's reported JND score is a regressor trained on internal user-study data; it is different from the deterministic attenuation mask. Reproducible observer-level data and a controlled F5 human evaluation are MISSING. Table2 means and the authors' visual examples are not an every-image or human-invisibility guarantee.

**K07 transfer, JUDGMENT:** native-resolution constraints plausibly address a failure mode that global L2 misses. The mask ablation also exposes a real strength-versus-visibility trade-off. No T3, independent-encoder, T4 or T5 evidence is supplied, and pixel-space edge concentration could suppress F5's transferable low-frequency carrier. Do not add this model as an external baseline or start adversarial training; use the mechanism only to specify a falsifiable local-constraint option.

## 7. Adversarial synthesis

**A. Budget shares are unidentified.** If successive actual residuals are `d1,...,dk`, the final squared error is `sum_i ||di||² + 2*sum_(i<j)<di,dj>`, plus any representation change not included in the saved stage definitions. Cross terms can help or hurt. A stage's configured PSNR allowance is neither its expenditure nor its causal marginal cost. Tang's second-layer example motivates stage measurement but supplies no transferable percentage for F5. A counterfactual writer must be rerun for removal/reallocation; subtraction from a finished image is not generally equivalent.

**B. Global L2 does not bound local visibility.** A fixed total norm permits concentrated residuals, low-frequency waves in smooth areas, or coherent color errors. SSIM/LPIPS averages can also conceal local defects. Pixel Seal gives a specific original-resolution spill mechanism, while its ablation shows that suppressing perturbations can reduce recovery. A JND mask estimates tolerance; it is neither a human response nor a theorem. F5 human acceptance and observer-tested flat patches stay MISSING.

**C. The old mask failure is real.** Repeating the same `mask_power=.5` plus a larger radius would relabel a failed package. A new mask must change a causal mechanism—e.g. original-resolution local caps and boundary handling—and demonstrate why it preserves the carrier. Texture can be a bad place for a mark that must survive a low-pass/foreign encoder. Multiplication by a mask also redistributes spectral energy; a spatial heuristic may destroy exactly the transferable component.

**D. Reallocation competes with complementary functions.** Gate-1's foreign-family second-tier hits prohibit “fragile equals useless” reasoning. The allocation objective must be valid owner/content decisions across T3/T4/T5, not robust-score magnitude or raw union hits. Conversely, complementarity alone does not justify keeping its present budget: false acceptance and final quality may still dominate. Compare retained full F5 and robust-only at matched final constraints before choosing.

**E. Channel/scale and quality definitions must remain separate.** VINE's fixed-kernel resolution effect is a concrete warning for Gate-1 N2. WAVES's attack-quality curves measure the price paid by an attacker; F5's clean thresholds measure the price paid by the owner. Neither can substitute for the other. SD1.4 versus SD2.1 labels do not establish independent VAE training; ROAR's table/text discrepancy adds uncertainty. None of these papers resolves F5's foreign-encoder validity gap.

**F. Reader recovery is a competing explanation.** ROAR shows that a reader bottleneck can improve without changing published pixels. Thus observed low detection does not prove that the writer needs more distortion. F5's normalized detector and bounded-cost reader differ from inversion-based methods; a literal ROAR port would change the method and computational claim. Its paper supplies a competing mechanism, not permission for that redesign.
