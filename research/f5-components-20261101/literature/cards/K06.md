### K06-J1 — MarkPlugger

SOURCE — Inspected §§III–V, equations 6–10, Tables I–II, channel/loss-weight/strength studies, and the discussion of failed earlier embedding positions; pp1–8 of the author PDF. No separate appendix was present. The method adds a learned watermark to one latent channel after denoising, freezes the generator/VAE, trains watermark encoder and decoder, and extracts through a learned pixel-space reader. Its training objective combines image MSE, VGG LPIPS and watermark losses, including a disturbed-image decoding term. Reported weights are 2, .2, 1, 1 and embedding strength .05. The claim of avoiding generator retraining does **not** mean that no watermark networks are trained.

SOURCE — Table I, COCO/SD1.5: PSNR **36.97 dB**, SSIM **94.46%**, LPIPS **.0424**, normalized correlation **96.78%**, character error rate **13.33%**. SDXL row: **36.83 dB / 94.02% / .0423 / 96.72% / 15.29%**. Section V-F varies embedding strength and loss weights; these show a quality/recovery tradeoff, not an isolated test of PGD iterations. Figure 8 includes examples with PSNR **34.97 and 34.53 dB**, below F5's strict >35 dB condition. Section V-I reports failure of earlier latent/noise insertion positions under its otherwise different architecture.

SOURCE — Threat tests include conventional corruptions and generation across model versions. **Generating and marking with SDXL is not regenerating an F5-marked image through an independently trained encoder.** Independent SDXL/K3 noisy-regeneration coverage is MISSING. T4 copy-paste ownership/content binding and T5 semantic-collision controls are MISSING. The learned reader uses its trained parameters; it does not establish F5's registry endpoint or lightweight fixed-reader properties. Aggregate quality metrics do not establish every-image strict quality or human invisibility.

JUDGMENT — Transferable idea: evaluate image quality after the nonlinear decoder and examine how loss weights change the feasible tradeoff. Failure mode for F5: this is amortized network training with another latent insertion point and reader, not a controlled test of F5 optimization. The 128-resolution setting uses 30 bits; the 256 setting uses 128 bits. Training is 151.2k steps/100 epochs with AdamW, learning rate 1e-4. This is not a training-free replacement for F5 PGD.

SOURCE — Equations 12–15 combine MSE plus .1 LPIPS with a message-index 2L-class cross-entropy objective. The authors report that their BCE/MSE alternatives did not converge in the 256 setting. **Table 5, p9**, with alpha=.1, is especially relevant: beta=.1 gives **48.1395 dB, SSIM .9946, LPIPS .0007, average BER 50.12%**; beta=1 gives **37.9254 dB, .9579, .0202, BER .92%**; the schedule beta=1→.1 gives **41.2869 dB, .9776, .0090, BER 1.75%**. Thus a quality-improving weight setting can erase the message, and a schedule trades some recovery for quality. This is source evidence about its objective, not evidence for a particular F5 schedule.

### K06-T1 — FreqMark

SOURCE — Inspected §§3–6, equations 1–11, Tables 1–7, Appendix A.1–A.4 including Tables 8–11, runtime/step discussion and quality-failure captions (PDF pp3–10,15–19). FreqMark optimizes a perturbation in the Fourier representation of an SD2.1 VAE latent and decodes a message through signs of fixed DINOv2-small image-feature projections. Networks are frozen. The loss combines a **linear hinge** margin objective on clean, latent-noised and pixel-noised outputs with **negative PSNR and LPIPS** penalties. This differs from F5's squared hinge plus hard RGB projection. The appendix specifies **Adam, learning rate 2.0, 400 steps**, PSNR weight **.05**, LPIPS weight **.25**, processing at **512×512**. Reported time is **5–6 minutes for two images in parallel** on A100 40 GB, fp32. It is not F5's per-image timing.

SOURCE — Table 1: ImageNet mean PSNR/SSIM **31.27/.857**, VAE-B/VAE-C/diffusion bit accuracy **.938/.924/.969**; DiffusionDB **31.20/.854**, corresponding accuracy **.925/.897/.945**. These means already fail F5's >35/.9 strict clean criteria. Table 3 compares optimization locations at similar mean PSNR around 31 dB: latent **.675** versus latent-frequency **.945** diffusion bit accuracy. The comparison is about parameterization/location and augmentation context, not an isolated optimizer-family intervention.

SOURCE — Table 4: diffusion steps **60→200** change bit accuracy **.945→.637** and attacked-image PSNR **27.67→23.57 dB**. Table 10: at nominal FPR 1e-3, TPR goes **1.000→.094** over those same step endpoints. Table 11's average over Gaussian noise plus three regeneration attacks rises from **.706** with no training noise to **.926** at latent-noise std .25 and pixel-noise std .06; this is an aggregate, not an independent-encoder-only result. Appendix A.3/Figure 7 discusses iteration dependence but provides no numerical table of per-step values; no digitized graph values are asserted here. Figure 11 expressly documents a moiré-like failure and semantic-background alterations.

SOURCE — The study uses 500 ImageNet validation images and 500 SD-generated DiffusionDB images, and separately larger panels for detection curves. Regeneration includes CompressAI Ballé/Cheng codecs and diffusion removal following its cited attack; own-VAE latent perturbations also appear. Independent learned-compression channels are present, but **SDXL/K3 family transfer under F5's attack definitions is MISSING**. The paper's diffusion-attack model/version is not sufficiently pinned in the inspected settings to classify it as F5's independent-encoder test. The binomial FPR calculation assumes independent balanced feature bits; near-zero pairwise correlations do not prove joint independence, and analytic low-FPR tails are not empirical T4/T5 safety. No record-bound copy-paste/collision test is supplied.

JUDGMENT — The paper supports inspecting parameterization, noisy objectives and quality/recovery tradeoffs. It does not show that 400 F5 steps are better than 200, that a feasible margin-4 solution exists at 52 dB, or that FFT coordinates alone improve the attainable solution. FFT is an invertible linear reparameterization absent additional constraints; observed algorithmic changes can depend on optimizer geometry and regularization. **The FreqMark retry line is closed.** This card is literature only. Any new FreqMark execution/baseline proposal must be explicitly labelled **REOPENS-CLOSED-LINE** and sent to the judge; none is proposed here.

### K06-T2 — END²

SOURCE — Inspected all nine author-version pages, especially Methodology/Algorithm 1 and equations 4–8 on pp3–4, experiments/Tables 1–5 on pp5–7; no appendix in this version. A teacher decoder sees clean watermarked images, a student sees truly distorted images, and the encoder receives gradients through the clean teacher branch. Feature vectors are projected and normalized, aligned with a cosine loss, and the decoders exchange roles each batch with momentum updates. This bypasses the nondifferentiable operation; it does **not** supply its exact gradient. Loss weights are **.01 / 8 / 5** for alignment/message/quality, momentum **.999**, Adam learning rate **8e-4**, 30 bits on 128×128 DIV2K crops, batch32, reported **5000 epochs** on RTX3090.

SOURCE — Table 4 ablates the components: no strategy **78.93% ACC / 45.04 dB / .9889 SSIM**; feature alignment alone **86.79% / 47.89 / .9921**; alignment+momentum **92.23% /39.26/.9620**; alignment+swapping **93.96% /43.68/.9867**; all three **94.55% /45.62/.9896**. Table 5 compares cosine alignment **94.55% /45.62 dB** against MSE **94.70% /42.92 dB** and DINO-style loss **91.39% /43.23 dB**. A recovery-only reading misses a 2.70 dB quality difference in the MSE comparison. Table 1 reports SSIM .9897 for the proposed model, while Tables 4–5 report .9896; preserve the table-specific values.

SOURCE — Experiments concern conventional distortions, real JPEG and four CycleGAN style transfers. Table 2 matches PSNR to **35**, which is not F5's strict **>35** per-image gate. Aggregate quality and example-image values do not establish a full strict-quality panel; LPIPS and human invisibility are MISSING. Independent diffusion encoder regeneration, T4 binding and T5 semantic collision are MISSING. Style-transfer operators are used during training for the relevant tests; a black-box implementation does not automatically imply an unseen attack family.

JUDGMENT — The relevant lesson is that losses and gradients through a convenient proxy must be checked against the real output channel, especially rounding/composition. A full dual-decoder method requires network training and is not a drop-in fixed-reader F5 optimizer. This card supports a gradient-faithfulness diagnostic and warns against assuming stop-gradient/straight-through approximations solve the forward objective. It establishes no F5 T3 gain.

### K06-T3 — ROAR

SOURCE — Inspected main §§3–5.6 and Tables 1–5 (pp19744–19749), the main conclusion, and supplement §§1–6, Tables1–2, loss-curve/qualitative captions. ROAR keeps the watermark writer fixed and improves **extraction**. A learned mixture-of-experts restoration network precedes DDIM inversion. Regeneration-based optimization then minimizes pixel MSE between an unconditional regeneration and the restored suspect image, optimizing the inferred initial latent. Defaults: **20 Adam iterations**, learning rate **5e-3→1e-6** cosine schedule, **50 inversion and reconstruction steps**, SD1.4/2.1, 512×512, 1000 generated images. The reader requires the diffusion model, VAE, inversion, restoration network and iterative regeneration, unlike F5's lightweight extraction objective.

SOURCE — Table 4 separates restoration from optimization. Example: Tree-Ring at Gaussian noise sigma=.2 has TPR **.101 base, .525 RO-only, .857 AR-only, 1.000 combined**. This isolates an optimization contribution in that detector but also shows that attributing the full package gain to RO would be wrong. Main Figure7/§5.6 reports diminishing gains after the selected 20 iterations; no graph-derived exact step-by-step values are claimed. Supplement §1 assumes local linearity of the denoiser and supports its argument with empirical distance histograms; it is not a global convergence or identifiability proof.

SOURCE — Table2 includes learned-codec reconstruction and diffusion purification. Purification at strengths **.1/.3/.5/.7** gives TPR **1.000/1.000/.980/.663**, bit accuracy **.987/.963/.855/.776**, attacked-image PSNR **23.79/20.15/18.75/16.65 dB**. **Source discrepancy:** §5.4 says the diffusion reconstruction attacker is SD-v2.1; Table2 labels the column **SD-v1.4**. The source is therefore not silently classified as a precisely identified independent-family experiment. No SDXL/K3 encoder transfer is established. A TPR set from a nominal analytic FPR of 1e-10 is not empirical proof at that rate. FID/CLIP generation metrics do not imply source-paired PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS or human invisibility. T4/T5 record binding is MISSING.

JUDGMENT — This is adjacent evidence that nonlinear latent reconstruction can benefit from a directly evaluated endpoint objective and then saturate. It is **not a writer intervention**, and cannot be used to claim K06 recovery, an F5 iteration optimum, or a lightweight detector improvement. The card is retained with that boundary because its optimizer/component ablations directly challenge simplistic extra-step explanations. Counting it as TOP for K06 remains a proposed relevance judgment.
