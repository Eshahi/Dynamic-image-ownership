## Decision context and adversarial synthesis

SOURCE — K11's inspected dossier identifies a public SHA-256-derived master, public OwnerID, domain-separated HMAC expansion and no private owner secret. OwnerID affects carriers/tables, but not the semantic projection rows. Native detection uses suspect RGB, pinned VAE, CLIP, profile and claimed owner; original q/h are evaluator information. The current reader has no registry. The inherited configuration hash does not identify every F5 weight, threshold and reader parameter; code locators are in the dossier's Structure section.

DERIVED — Public deterministic expansion can separate streams but cannot authenticate possession of a private key. The frozen native reader therefore supports a statistical, owner-conditioned decision under its stated attack construction, not a cryptographic ownership proof. A random-null tail bound does not bound an adaptive adversary who chooses an image using the public detector. This is a limit on the claim, not a new measured attack on F5.

JUDGMENT — The literature changes the assumptions audit more strongly than the regeneration design. Gaussian Shading's distribution-preservation argument and the later transfer-forgery results can both be true. The latter experiments use fresh keys/nonces, so attributing the problem solely to key reuse is unjustified. Conversely, those attacks do not establish a strict-quality F5 T4/T5 break: their reader, mark, attack process and quality budget differ. The CCS iris paper supplies a useful conditional-entropy requirement, not a recipe for generating an ownership secret from an already published image. The journal papers expose concrete distinctions among a secret source, authenticated enrollment, signature verification and perceptual matching.

DERIVED — Let X be the public published image and f the public deterministic feature extraction. Then H-infinity(f(X) given X,f)=0. For a fuzzy extractor that reconstructs a key from f(X) and public helper data, an attacker possessing X can run the same reconstruction. Randomness in enrollment does not rescue this if the public helper plus the public image suffice for legitimate recovery. This argument is about this proposed use, not an impossibility theorem for fuzzy extractors with private biometrics or physical secrets.

JUDGMENT — Three assumptions must remain separate: (1) an unforgeable signature binds bytes to an authenticated verification key; (2) enrollment policy binds that key to an owner and image record; (3) a noisy content matcher links a suspect to that record. Neither (1) nor fuzzy tolerance proves (2) or (3). A copied valid signature is still valid; whether it should authenticate a different image is a content-binding question. Legal ownership and license management remain outside the thesis.

### K11-GS24 — Gaussian Shading

SOURCE — Zijin Yang, Kai Zeng, Kejiang Chen, Han Fang, Weiming Zhang and Nenghai Yu. “Gaussian Shading: Provable Performance-Lossless Image Watermarking for Diffusion Models.” CVPR 2024, pp.12162–12171. Canonical URL and PDF above; DOI not needed for identification and not independently verified here. Class **TOP**, venue V-GS; citation key `yang2024gaussian`. Inspected main §§3–6, pp.3–8, Tables 1–3, and supplement §§1–2, pp.1–5, including proofs and supplementary experiments.

SOURCE — Mechanism: repeat payload, encrypt the repeated bits with ChaCha20 and select equiprobable Gaussian intervals for initial diffusion noise; invert the image, decrypt and majority-decode. The §3.4/supplement §1 argument concerns computational indistinguishability under cipher/key/nonce assumptions and the induced distribution, not per-image fidelity or cryptographic content binding. The operator has secret encryption material and inversion access. Main §4.1 uses SD1.4/2.0/2.1, 512-pixel images, 50 denoising and inversion steps and 256 payload bits. Table 1 reports average TPR .997/.998/.996 across its nine perturbations at theoretical FPR 1e-6. Those standard-distortion numbers are secondary, not a T3 verdict. Supplement §1.1 uses an independent-bit null; §2.1's 50,000 negatives cannot empirically establish ultra-small tails. Main §4.5 explicitly studies framing through inversion and changed prompts; §5 acknowledges the associated risk.

JUDGMENT — Transferable: specify secret material, freshness, owner search size, null assumptions and attacker access separately. Not transferable: “performance-lossless” as a strict-quality guarantee for F5, or ciphertext-like marginals as unforgeability. Its inversion-based reader/writer is not F5's lightweight extraction interface. **Independent-encoder regeneration:** main regeneration/compression and inversion-framing tests do not establish F5's matched independent-encoder img2img gate. F5 cost and C/J improvement: MISSING. Confidence is high for the restricted proof interpretation, not for a security guarantee under the thesis attacker.

### K11-BF25 — Black-box semantic-watermark forgery

### K11-FE25 — Practical fuzzy extraction from private iris measurements

SOURCE — Amey Shukla, Luke Demarest, Benjamin Fuller, Sohaib Ahmad, Caleb Manicke, Alexander Russell and Sixia Chen. “Fuzzy Extractors are Practical: Cryptographic Strength Key Derivation from the Iris.” ACM CCS 2025, pp.3605–3619, DOI [10.1145/3719027.3765098](https://doi.org/10.1145/3719027.3765098). SOURCE — Sample-then-lock wraps a random key in many digital lockers using selected noisy feature subsets. The scheme filters weak subsets and weights sampling by feature quality. §4 corrects an earlier argument: the minimum subset min-entropy matters, not its average. Assumption 1 models the security of digital lockers; the HMAC instantiation relies on the stated idealization. DERIVED/JUDGMENT — Public-image feature entropy conditioned on the image is zero under the model above. Do not transplant its iris feature architecture or reopen a closed reference-free descriptor family. Confidence high for this boundary; feasibility as an F5 improvement is unsupported.
