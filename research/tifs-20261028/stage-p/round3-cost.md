## Proposed planning ceiling and what it means

Offer the user **126 paid instance-hours** as a provisional ceiling for the full maximum Stage P plus amended Stage S package, subject to measured resource qualification. This is the smallest whole-hour ceiling covering the explicitly declared **middle scenario** below: 125.8175 hours rounded upward. It is **not** an empirically established minimum, a promise that the work fits, or the only reasonable budget. The fast/middle/stress assumptions yield **62/126/265 hours** after independently allowing for budget-control writer cost. These are sensitivity scenarios, not a confidence interval. They cover the current K3-out design; deferred K3-in gradients are not priced here.

At the brief's **assumed** USD .45-.60/hour, the three caps imply **USD 27.90-37.20 / 56.70-75.60 / 119.25-159.00**, excluding separately billed storage/network. These rates are not a current quote. Use the actual price and the user-approved ceiling on the server. All paid setup, CPU work, idle time, failed work, offload transfers, checkpoint recomputation and collection count. On one reserved device, instance-hours are billed GPU reservation-hours; they do not imply full GPU utilization.

The ideal minimum whole-hour reservation, conditional on a complete measured remaining-work forecast and the chosen allowance, is instead

```text
ceil((E + F_remaining + 1.5 * W_remaining + 3600) / 3600) hours
```

where E is **all paid wall time already elapsed**, F_remaining is remaining fixed setup/freeze work, W_remaining is a complete measured forecast for remaining work including recurring loads and diagnostics, and 3600 seconds is collection reserve. In this ideal nonduplicating calculation, explicitly accounted completed calibration/rehearsal work enters E and is deducted from W_remaining. No elapsed work, including failed attempts, is reset out of the clock. The 1.5 factor is an engineering allowance, not a bound on all possible hardware/source variability; even measured inputs do not establish a guaranteed completion time.

**Runtime accounting is deliberately more conservative than that ideal minimum.** The current Stage P forecast reserves the entire maximum allocation of 136 marked writes plus 136 C0 source-directory units after calibration, while E already includes calibration. This over-reserves time; it does not instruct the runtime to repeat completed scientific outputs. Calibration identities are still reused. Its frozen forecast is not automatically recomputed after every rung. Per-unit paid-deadline/collection-reserve checks remain active. Full-allocation reservation, operation-bound proxies and repeated load allowances can reject a 126-hour choice even when this scenario rounds to 126; the runtime's resource stop is authoritative. This document does not remove those allowances or promise a more efficient runtime forecast.

Specifically, the current runtime charges all 72 candidate writes at the slower measured noise/x0 time and all 40 budget-control writes at the maximum of that candidate proxy and retained F5. It also reserves repeated complete model-load cycles alongside measured overhead. These are deliberate conservative reservations, unlike this scenario's feasible 56/16 candidate allocation and independent budget-control timing. The budget proxy is not an empirical bound on its checkpointed implementation. If the operational forecast needs more than 126 hours, the outcome is RESOURCE_STOP or a new user budget decision; no cap expands automatically.

There are two distinct resource decisions on the same paid clock. Stage P's first-two-source calibration qualifies **remaining Stage P only**; it cannot establish VINE/WAM, Stage S threats/secondary, full binding-reader or all-arm costs. After a successful Stage P check and freeze, the separate full Stage S rehearsal must qualify the intact screen within the same approved total cap. If it cannot, stop after P and the rehearsal with RESOURCE_STOP, retain all evidence and return it. Do not start a fractional screen, reduce arms/sources or label missing S work a scientific negative. Approval of a 126-hour ceiling does not bypass either measured qualification.

## Gradient, replay and decoding work that must not disappear

Across maximum P plus complete S, before diagnostic checks:

- **70,400 reader-gradient evaluations:** 144 F6 writes at 350 each, 58 budget-F5 writes at 200 each, and 42 retained-F5 writes at 200 each.
- **18,900 codec gradient graphs:** 126 noise/x0/codec writes at 150 each; R0 accounts for 6,300 and R1 for 12,600. R1 codec offload/reload cost accompanies those 12,600 graphs in the current implementation.
- **2,700 generic-transform gradient graphs:** eighteen generic writes at 150 each.
- **5,100-11,100 native UNet differentiated graphs**, depending on P survivors; the upper allocation has 3,700 R0 and 7,400 R1 graphs. Each graph includes forward, backward and checkpoint recomputation. The current per-update offload policy also makes up to 11,100 UNet CPU/GPU round trips relevant.

These are components of a writer's elapsed time, not independent complete writer runs to add again after measuring the full embed. No backward speed is inferred from a forward-only native trajectory. Frozen weights and activation checkpointing reduce different memory terms; checkpointing does not establish fit and may make execution slower. The rehearsal must verify peak against measured total minus 1.5 GiB, including load peaks, and enforce the same device policy on resume. Cached CPU weights and transfer buffers also require measured host RAM and swap checks.

Additional diagnostics are explicitly charged:

- P gradient calibration: four clean-reader probes and twelve surrogate-reader probes, twelve codec graphs, six complete x0 gradient graphs and six extra epsilon-input backward probes. There are four variant-specific source invocations.
- S rehearsal: two clean-reader probes and twenty-four surrogate-reader probes, eighteen codec graphs, six generic graphs, six complete x0 graphs and six extra epsilon-input backward probes. There are two source invocations covering all four F6 variants.
- Both stages together: twelve projection-parity reader forwards, plus feature extraction and diagnostic bookkeeping. Their time is included in the per-invocation diagnostic allowance.
- C0 RGB8 replay parity adds **72 full native trajectory executions in P calibration and 24 in S rehearsal**, beyond the saved native attempt counts. These 96 replays produce no extra scientific identity. Each native cell receives 24 extra replay executions across the two stages.
- First-step x0 parity adds **18 native epsilon forwards in P** and **six in S**, using the actual native C0 trajectory without adding another saved trajectory. There are eight R0 and sixteen R1 extra epsilon forwards in total.

The diagnostic x0 path differentiates both its reader loss and its epsilon-input probe. The cost arithmetic conservatively charges a full UNet graph for each backward, including an over-allowance for forward/transfer reuse; measured full diagnostic times supersede that proxy. The cost assumption matches calibration in all three P directories. Any change to probe reuse requires an explicit accounting correction before execution, never silently subtracting measured cost.

To distinguish physical operations: the saved native inventory contains 5,640 forward trajectories; parity adds 96 further full forward trajectories and 24 standalone epsilon forwards. These are separate from writer differentiation. Diagnostics add twelve x0 reader-loss backward graphs and twelve extra epsilon-input backwards to the writer's 5,100-11,100 denoiser-gradient graphs. Actual checkpoint recomputation forwards are implementation-dependent and included in each measured graph duration; they are not claimed to have a known multiplicity from these logical counts.

There are 10,592 internal detector rows, 1,008 each for WAM/VINE-R/VINE-B, and 5,292 additional internal binding-mode calls on threat rows. Internal row assumptions include both owner calls sharing their native features. VINE-B has 2,016 owner decodes, each exhausting 41,449 q candidates: **83,561,184 candidate checks**. Decoder CPU time and all reader features count even if they occur while the GPU is idle. Known-message wrong-message comparison for WAM/VINE-R shares the native message decode; no second network pass is assumed.
