Abstract
Digital watermarking is a cornerstone for copyright pro-
tection. With the rapid advancement of generative models
like diffusion models, in-generation and training-free wa-
termarking techniques have garnered more attention for
their endogeneity and convenience. These methods typi-
cally embed a watermark into the initial noise, where wa-
termark extraction relies on Denoising Diffusion Implicit
Models (DDIM) inversion. However , the computationally
intensive extraction process severely hinders their path to-
ward practical deployment. To overcome this critical bot-
tleneck, we propose GROW, a novel training-free paradigm
that reframes watermarking from a one-shot “embedding”
to a progressive “growth”. By progressively guiding using
frequency-domain gradients, GROW naturally weaves the
watermark into the image, which enables inversion-free ex-
traction. Comprehensive experiments on multiple datasets
show that GROW not only achieves superior robustness and
imperceptibility but also offers a detection speed nearly
100x faster than inversion-based techniques. The code will
be made publicly available.
1. Introduction
The rapid advancement of generative models, particularly
diffusion models (DMs) [19, 29, 33, 36, 37] has revolution-
ized AI-generated content (AIGC), enabling the creation of
photorealistic images from textual prompts. This accessi-
bility, however, brings significant challenges in copyright
protection and content traceability [5, 22]. Digital water-
marking [20] is a critical technology to address these is-
sues by embedding imperceptible information into gener-
ated images, thereby providing a means for authentication
and provenance tracking.
Existing watermarking methods for diffusion models can
be broadly classified into three categories.Post-processing

A seminal work in this area, Tree-Ring [38], embeds wa-

We realize that the solution lies not in abandoning the
principle of endogeneity, but in rethinking its mechanism.
The robustness of initial-noise-based methods stems from a
passive scatteringprocess, where the denoising model im-
plicitly filters a watermarked noise, fusing a portion of it
with the image semantics. Our key insight is to achieve
this same desirable fusion throughactive guidance. As
depicted in Figure 1(b), we bridge image generation and
watermark generation by leveraging the model’s own it-
erative denoising process. Instead of modifying the ini-
tial noise, we introduce a gradient-based guidance mech-
anism. At each denoising step, we compute the difference
between the latent features (in the frequency domain) and a
fixed target watermark pattern. The gradient of this differ-
ence is then used to gently steer the generation, constitut-
ing a progressive guidance approach. The subtle perturba-
tions introduced at each step accumulate along the model’s
learned data manifold. In subsequent steps, the U-Net de-
noiser, driven by the conditioning signal, naturally incorpo-
rates these perturbations into the emerging image textures
like feathers and tree bark, resulting in a deeply fused wa-
termark.
This reformulation forms the core of our method,
GROW(Generation with progressiveWatermarking).
Crucially, it retains the semantic fusion advantage of prior
work while offering a decisive practical benefit: extrac-
tion is direct, efficient, and completely free from inversion.
GROW thus establishes a new paradigm, shifting from a
static ”imprint” to a dynamic ”co-evolution” between the
image and the watermark.
Our work makes three key contributions:
• We propose GROW, a novel training-free paradigm that
reframes watermarking as a progressive guidance pro-
cess, eliminating the dependency on costly model inver-
sion for extraction.
• We develop a novel mechanism that leverages gradient
guidance in the frequency domain to weave the water-
mark into the final latent representations.
• We empirically demonstrate that GROW not only sur-
passes existing methods in robustness and imperceptibil-
ity but also achieves a nearly 100x speedup in extraction,
offers a fast and practical watermarking solution.
2. Related Works
Recent research on image watermarking can be broadly cat-
egorized into three categories.
Post-processing-based: These watermarking algo-
rithms [6, 7, 35, 41, 44] are applied directly to the final gen-
erated images. These methods, often adapted from classical
techniques [35, 44], typically operate in the spatial domain,
a transform domain (e.g., DCT, DWT) or a latent space to
embed the watermark signal. For example, RivaGAN [41]
uses a GAN-based framework with an encoder-decoder ar-
chitecture to embed and extract watermarks.
Fine-tuning-based: To achieve deeper integration, ap-
proaches amalgamate the watermarking process with the
model’s architecture [4, 8, 10, 11, 13, 14, 24, 27, 28, 31,
39, 43]. These approaches typically modify a pre-trained
diffusion model to instill a watermarking capability. For
instance, Stable Signature [14] fine-tunes the V AE decoder
with a dedicated watermark extractor, enabling the decoder
to implicitly embed a signature during image reconstruc-
tion. Other works [27, 43] propose fine-tuning a specific
trigger to hidden watermark. Some methods also intro-
duce the watermark as a conditional input during the train-
ing phase [39]. Although these methods can produce high-
quality watermarked images, they require significant com-
putational resources for retraining and result in modified
model parameters, which can be a major barrier for prac-
tical deployment.
Training-free: Recently, training-free methods [2, 3, 9,
15, 21, 23, 25, 34, 40] have emerged as a flexible, plug-
and-play alternative. Their dominant strategy, as illustrated
in Figure 1(a), is to manipulate the initial noise latentz T .
The seminal work, Tree-Ring [38], demonstrated that wa-
termark information can be embedded in the initial noise.
This foundational idea has inspired a lineage of research.
For instance, WIND [2] focuses on designing specific pat-
terns for improved robustness, while SEAL [3] innovatively
links the watermark to the semantic content of the input
prompt. Gaussian Shading [40] proposes mapping the wa-
termark to a distribution-preserving Gaussian representa-
tion to achieve performance-lossless embedding. Despite
their ingenuity, a critical flaw shared by all these initial-
noise-based methods is their reliance on a computationally
expensive inversion process [36] for watermark detection.
Recovering the initial noise latent from a given image is a
time-consuming procedure that severely hinders the prac-

3.2. Guidance in Diffusion Models
Guidance techniques are fundamental to controlling the
generation process in diffusion models.
Classifier Guidance[12], an early approach, steers gen-
eration by incorporating gradients from a pre-trained clas-
sifier pϕ(y|zt) into the noise prediction:
ˆE=E θ(zt, t)−σ t∇zt logp ϕ(y|zt) (2)
where Eθ(zt, t) is the model’s predicted noise, σt is the
noise schedule, and ∇zt logp ϕ(y|zt) is the gradient of the
classifier with respect to the latent zt for class y. While
powerful, this method requires training a separate classifier
on noisy data and can compromise sample diversity.
Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)[18] has become the
mainstream approach by circumventing the need for a sep-
arate classifier. It achieves this by jointly training the dif-
fusion model on conditional and unconditional inputs and
interpolating their noise predictions during inference:
ˆECFG =E θ(zt, t,∅) +w·(E θ(zt, t, c)− E θ(zt, t,∅)) (3)
Here, Eθ(zt, t, c) is the conditional prediction (with prompt
c), Eθ(zt, t,∅) is the unconditional prediction (with empty
prompt ∅), and w >1 is the guidance scale that amplifies
the direction pointed by the conditional prediction. This
allows for flexible control over the trade-off between sample
quality and adherence to the condition.
Our work is conceptually inspired by these guidance
paradigms. We adapt the core idea of steering the denoising
trajectory to the novel task of invisible watermarking. In-
stead of guiding semantic attributes like ”an astronaut rid-
ing a horse,” we guide the generation process to subtly and
progressively incorporate a watermark signal, leveraging a
similar mechanism to achieve deep fusion within the natural
image manifold.
4. Method
4.1. Overview
Our proposed method, GROW, introduces a new paradigm
for training-free watermarking. Instead of embedding a
static pattern into the initial noise, we progressively guide
the generation process towards a predefined watermark tar-
get. This is achieved by treating watermarking as an opti-
mization problem solved concurrently with denoising. The
overall framework is depicted in Figure 2.
4.2. GROW
4.2.1. Frequency-Domain Watermark Encoding
To create a watermark that is both imperceptible and robust,
we embed it in the frequency domain of the latent space us-
ing the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT). We strategically
choose to embed the watermark in the mid-frequency band.
This choice is crucial: embedding in low-frequency com-
ponents would visibly distort the core structure of the im-
age, while embedding in high-frequency components would
make the watermark vulnerable to removal by common
operations like JPEG compression or blurring. The mid-
frequency band thus offers an optimal trade-off between vi-
sual quality and robustness.
First, a secret messageWis converted into a binary bit
sequencew. Given a secret keyK, we use a seeded pseudo-
random number generator to generate a binary mask matrix
M. The bit sequencewis repeated and mapped to mask

4.2.3. Watermark Extraction
The extraction process, summarized in Algorithm 1, re-
quires no inversion. Given a potentially watermarked image
x, we first obtain its latent representationz 0 using the V AE
encoder. We then apply the DCT toz 0 to get its frequency
representationF.
Concurrently, the secret keyKis used to deterministi-
cally regenerate the binary maskM, which identifies the ex-
act frequency locations where the watermark bits were em-
bedded. For each location(u, v)where the maskM(u, v)is
1, we extract a single bit by checking the sign of the corre-
sponding DCT coefficientF(u, v): a positive sign decodes
to ’1’, and a negative sign decodes to ’0’. Since each bit was
repeated multiple times, we use a majority vote to determine
the final value for each bit, enhancing the robustness of the
extraction. Finally, the collected sequence of bits is con-
verted back into the messageW.

5. Experiments
5.1. Datasets and Metrics
Datasets. To ensure a comprehensive evaluation, we con-
duct tests on 1,000 generated images for each method from
two widely-used datasets: MS-COCO [26] for photoreal-
istic images and Stable-Diffusion-Prompts [16] for artistic
and complex concepts.
Metrics. We evaluate performance from two perspec-
tives:Imperceptibility.We measure the visual quality of
watermarked images using PSNR, SSIM, Fr´echet Inception
Distance (FID) [17], and LPIPS [42]. For PSNR, SSIM,
higher is better. For FID, LPIPS, lower is better.
Robustness to attacks.We evaluate the robustness of
the embedded watermark by measuring the Message Accu-
racy (M-ACC) after applying various attacks. M-ACC is a
strict metric that calculates the percentage of images from
which the entire embedded message is recovered without
any bit errors. This is significantly more challenging than
Bit Accuracy and more meaningful for practical applica-
tions where message integrity is paramount. The attacks
include Rotation, JPEG compression, Cropping, Gaussian
Blur, Gaussian Noise, Brightness adjustment and adversar-
ial attack Diff-Pure [30] (illustrated in Figure 3).
5.2. Implementation Details
All experiments are performed using the Stable Diffusion
v2.1-base [33] model. We compare GROW against a wide
range of watermarking methods, including post-processing
based (DwtDctSvd [35], Hidden [44]) and other training-
free methods (Tree-Ring [38], RingID [9], Gaussian Shad-
ing [40], WIND [2]).
All experiments are conducted using the PyTorch 1.13.0
framework, running on a single Tesla T4 GPU. For our
method, we set the default parameters toα= 0.5,η= 100,
sample 50 steps andr start = 0.5to test. And our exper-
iments embed a 16-bit watermark in the first channel of
the V AE features. However, our framework is flexible, and
other channels can also be utilized to embed watermarks,
either independently or concurrently, allowing for higher-
capacity watermarking.

efficiency gain makes GROW a viable and practical solu-
tion for deployment in production environments where fast
verification is essential.
5.4. Different Diffusion Models
To assess the generalizability of our approach, we evaluated
GROW and other methods on both stable-diffusion-v1-5
[32] and the more advanced stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0 [1]
(SDXL) model. The comprehensive results in Table 2 con-
firm that GROW’s advantages are model-agnostic. Across
both architectures, GROW consistently achieves a balance
of high imperceptibility and robustness. This suggests that
the progressive guidance mechanism is a fundamental and
portable technique for watermarking diffusion models.

5.6. Ablation Study on Progressive Guidance
To validate the necessity of our progressive guidance mech-
anism, we conduct a crucial ablation study against a sim-
plified ”one-step” baseline. This baseline injects the same
DCT-based watermark pattern directly into the final latent
space (z 0) just before decoding, using an identical water-
mark strength. The results are striking. As shown in the
visual comparison in Figure 6, the one-step method com-
pletely corrupts the image content. We quantify this degra-
dation on MS-COCO dataset using standard image quality
metrics in Table 4. The one-step method leads to a catas-
trophic collapse in image quality, with FID soaring while
PSNR and SSIM plummet. This experiment unequivocally
demonstrates that progressively integrating the watermark
is essential for embedding a strong signal without a disas-
trous loss of image quality.
6. Limitations and Future Work
Despite its strong performance, GROW, as a training-free
method in the DCT domain, has limitations. Its robustness
can be compromised by random ratio stretching, and its ca-
pacity is inherently constrained by the frequency domain
structure. A promising future direction is to replace the
static DCT domain with a learnable, attack-resilient repre-

generate a robust watermark embedding space optimized

7. Conclusion
We proposed GROW, a novel and effective training-free wa-
termarking paradigm for diffusion models. By reformulat-
ing watermark embedding as a progressive guidance task,
our method iteratively guides the generation to form a wa-
termark throughout the denoising process. This multi-step
integration strategy ensures that the watermark is deeply
fused with the generated content, achieving an excellent
balance between imperceptibility and robustness. Extensive
experiments validate that GROW significantly surpasses ex-
isting methods, demonstrating resilience to a wide range of
attacks while preserving the high fidelity of the original dif-
fusion model. Furthermore, by bypassing the computation-
ally expensive inversion step required by many prior meth-
ods, GROW enables highly efficient watermark extraction.
