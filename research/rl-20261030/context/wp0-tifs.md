## Gate limitations to carry into later RL comparisons

1. **SOURCE — empirical screen versus mechanism claim.** Both profiles preserve C/J foreign retention at least 12/16, gains 4/4/3 against retained F5, original-family loss at most one, strict candidate quality on every source and clean F at least 15/16. The proposed profile adds F5-budget quality/F eligibility and 4/4/3 gains plus paired quality noninferiority against it, but moves ablation superiority and eligibility to secondary outcomes. Thus it relinquishes automatic superiority over mechanism controls and near-quality parity to retained F5 (`TD/09-round3-amendment.md`, 29–31; `TD/design/r3-gate-analysis.md`, profile table 104–119).
2. **SOURCE — no equal-compute conclusion.** F5-budget has 200 reader gradients; F6 has 350 plus surrogate work. Nominal robust-radius matching does not equal final PSNR/LPIPS, SSIM, spatial distortion or human quality. Matched achieved-quality frontiers would require separate prospective calibration. This inherited control cannot by itself answer the RL brief's equal-compute question (`TD/design/r3-gate-analysis.md`, “Budget matching is not measured quality matching,” 123–129; `TD/FINAL-REPORT.md`, 61).
3. **SOURCE — surrogate contrast is confounded.** F6-x0 changes both denoiser inclusion and timestep exposure relative to F6-noise. One-step parity and a finite nonzero gradient do not validate the whole attack gradient or final reader survival. The local derivative `I - b_t J_f` is algebra about that surrogate, not a robustness theorem (`TD/design/r3-surrogate-prior-art.md`, 37–54).
4. **SOURCE — transfer exposure.** K3 is historically observed and selection-seen, though gradient-excluded. It is not untouched unseen-family evidence. All 60 current development images have historical exposure (`TD/FINAL-REPORT.md`, 57; `TD/design/r3-gate-analysis.md`, “K3,” 133–135). This extraction does not open or enumerate the prospective confirmation pool.
5. **DERIVED in the earlier file — source count and dependence.** A 12/16 screen does not establish population retention of 75%; the earlier one-sided exact 95% lower bound is about .5156. Three seeds do not turn n=16 into n=48. J implies C for valid references, so treating their absolute gates as independent is wrong, although J comparative gains do not imply C gains. Seed, source and cross-cell correlations are distinct (`TD/design/r3-gate-analysis.md`, 13–27, 43–53, 55–72).
6. **DERIVED in the earlier file — no identified whole-gate probability.** Foreign retention alone does not supply the joint distributions for quality, clean F, negative events, R0 retention, pairing and missingness. The finite-sum grids assume hypothetical rates and dependence; they are not fitted F6 forecasts. In the toy example, a strong ablation can kill mechanism superiority despite useful candidate retention (`TD/design/r3-gate-analysis.md`, 23–27, 76–100).
7. **SOURCE — VINE-B article screen stays separate.** Both endpoints may lose at most one source per cell versus VINE-B, with paired median LPIPS increase at most .005 and PSNR decrease at most 1 dB. A comparative edge additionally requires three-source gains on both endpoints in one foreign cell, or the stated .002 LPIPS and 1-dB PSNR quality advantage. These are development margins, not inferential noninferiority tests. VINE-R presence cannot predict VINE-B blind q/tag/h recovery (`TD/FINAL-REPORT.md`, 65–67; `TD/design/r3-gate-analysis.md`, 139–143).
8. **SOURCE — specificity and attribution.** Complete qualifying C0/wrong-owner and T4/T5 negatives are required. Missing human distinctness labels leave binding generalization unresolved. Zero events in 16 source units still allows an illustrative one-sided 95% error upper bound about 17.1%; many queries on the same images are not independent negatives (`TD/04-selection.md`, 39–40, 50; `TD/design/r3-gate-analysis.md`, 100). Public-derived owner checks are redundancy, not cryptographic authentication (`TD/FINAL-REPORT.md`, 67).
