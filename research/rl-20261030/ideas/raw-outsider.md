## RO01 — Compiler pass ordering inside the writer

**Analogy:** compiler autotuning, where one transformation changes which later transformations are useful. **Class/point:** R-full; **P3**, pre-encoder transforms in the writer loop. **State/action/feedback:** observe the current residual, reader margins, gradient conflicts and preceding operations; choose the next surrogate, noise step or transform, then update through its ordinary gradient. Terminal feedback is native C/J and final clean quality. Transformations are training tools; ordinary distortions acquire no new threat verdict. **Why it might beat non-RL:** a policy could exploit noncommuting operations and delayed recovery that a fixed order or greedy next-loss choice misses; compare beam search and equally informed schedule optimization too. **Biggest unknown:** whether operation history predicts terminal benefit beyond current losses and whether gradients already remove the ordering dependence.

## RO02 — Algorithm portfolio before any mark exists

**Analogy:** instance-specific solver selection in scheduling or combinatorial optimization. **Class/point:** R-bandit; **P1**, before marking. **State/action/feedback:** use clean-source features available without making candidate marks; choose one registered writer configuration or quality budget; observe only that choice's native C/J, constraints and total cost during future development training. **Why it might beat non-RL:** partial feedback and reusable source structure could make exploration across a new configuration menu valuable compared with a best fixed configuration or a supervised selector trained on the same observations. **Biggest unknown:** action-dependent heterogeneity may be absent. WP3's retained-arm ceiling does not support assuming that a new menu has headroom; easy-to-predict survival is insufficient.

## RO03 — Place carrier resources like chip blocks

**Analogy:** chip placement with congestion and scarce routing resources. **Class/point:** R-full; **P2**, spatial/frequency allocation before the encoder. **State/action/feedback:** represent assigned carrier groups, remaining amplitude budget, spectral occupancy and predicted interference; successively allocate the next group to a permitted region/band with a selected amplitude. Later placements alter the remaining feasible choices. Native C/J and clean constraints give terminal feedback. **Why it might beat non-RL:** a sequential placement policy might learn interactions that independent saliency masks or greedy allocation miss; compare discrete optimization and differentiable placement. **Biggest unknown:** whether meaningful placement freedom exists with the fixed F5 reader. Moving code locations outside that contract is explicitly a new carrier/reader design, requiring its own comparison and binding tests.

## RO04 — Probe the surrogate before trusting it

**Analogy:** robotics dual control, where a small action both controls and identifies a system. **Class/point:** R-full; **P3/P4**. **State/action/feedback:** maintain uncertainty about local surrogate responses; choose a bounded reversible probe, a normal gradient update or a return to a saved state. Probe responses update the belief used for subsequent writer actions. Train terminal value against native responses on training sources only. **Why it might beat non-RL:** a deliberately informative probe could improve later choices when a greedy descent step cannot reveal model mismatch; compare model-predictive control and classical experiment design. **Biggest unknown:** cheap local probes may identify only the surrogate, not the unknown regenerator. Any native probe at writing time is extra simulator access, charged and shared with comparators, not deployment-channel knowledge.

## RO05 — Curriculum on the boundary of learnability

**Analogy:** sim-to-real training curricula and adaptive task generation. **Class/point:** R-game; **P3/P8**. **State/action/feedback:** a teacher observes training-only progress and uncertainty by channel mechanism; it chooses the next surrogate mixture or difficulty, while a writer learner responds. Teacher feedback rewards useful subsequent native improvement, rather than merely finding the largest current failure. **Why it might beat non-RL:** a responsive curriculum could preserve informative gradients between easy saturation and complete erasure better than a fixed/random mixture; compare progress-based heuristic sampling and robust randomized gradients. **Biggest unknown:** progress can be transient simulator exploitation. A family excluded from the entire curriculum is still needed, and this analogy supplies no automatic unseen-family guarantee.

## RO06 — Residual control around an existing gradient optimizer

**Analogy:** residual robotics control, with a conventional controller handling ordinary motion and a learned controller correcting specific failure modes. **Class/point:** R-full; **P4**. **State/action/feedback:** keep ordinary pathwise writer updates; expose only low-dimensional corrections such as trust-region level, accept/rollback or surrogate-switch decisions. Observe predicted versus realized local improvement, clean margins and update history; learn from terminal native C/J. **Why it might beat non-RL:** delayed corrections could avoid locally productive but transfer-destructive updates; compare trust-region rules, line search and an equally informed supervised correction policy. **Biggest unknown:** there may be no observable early signature of later native failure, leaving the learned correction to memorize training channels.

## RO07 — Assembly planning for robust and fragile components

**Analogy:** robotic assembly, where operations can obstruct or disturb previously installed parts. **Class/point:** R-full; **P9/P4**. **State/action/feedback:** track robust and fragile margins, residual geometry, quantization status and available repair operations; choose bounded robust-write, fragile-write, rounding, repair and stop actions. Reward final native C/J while retaining the original payload and separate forgery criteria. **Why it might beat non-RL:** contingent repair sequences could outperform a single fixed ordering when interference differs by source; compare coordinate descent, enumerated schedules and greedy repair. **Biggest unknown:** actual interference may be negligible or unrelated to regeneration failure. Optimizing an easier content code is disallowed; changing its semantics would create a different method.

## RO08 — Spend three quality budgets as a constrained controller

**Analogy:** industrial control with several simultaneous resource constraints. **Class/point:** R-full; **P1/P4**. **State/action/feedback:** start with a registered budget envelope, then observe remaining PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS slack plus reader margins; choose the next allowed refinement mode and its intensity. Final saved-image quality is a hard feasibility condition, not an average reward tradeoff. **Why it might beat non-RL:** learning the future quality consequences of different operations could allocate slack more effectively than one scalar radius; compare constrained gradients, Pareto search and a rule-based controller. **Biggest unknown:** intermediate quality signals may not predict final RGB8 feasibility, and any apparent advantage could come from the constraint representation rather than RL.

## RO09 — Active sensing with a stopping certificate

**Analogy:** active sonar or medical sensing that chooses the next measurement instead of collecting every view. **Class/point:** R-full; **P5**. **State/action/feedback:** use the suspect, claimed owner and already permitted observations; select a next view or terminate with accept/reject/abstain. Training feedback combines decision correctness and measurement use under a separately calibrated sequential error rule. This is a verifier modification. **Why it might beat non-RL:** conditional view value could differ after an ambiguous first observation; compare fixed cascades and classical information-gain policies. **Biggest unknown:** valid conditional evidence may be unavailable because views are dependent and policy-selected. Summing reader scores or treating views as independent cannot supply the certificate.

## RO10 — Learn which diagnostic experiment resolves the mechanism

**Analogy:** fault diagnosis in an engineered system, where tests distinguish competing failure mechanisms. **Class/point:** R-bandit; **P8**. **State/action/feedback:** represent uncertainty among carrier, reader and channel-stage explanations; choose a prospective controlled diagnostic, such as a matched carrier/reader comparison or an intervention at one regeneration stage. Feedback is prespecified reduction of predictive disagreement on separate fresh development observations. **Why it might beat non-RL:** context-dependent test value could identify the bottleneck with fewer uninformative measurements; compare balanced factorial designs and classical Bayesian design. **Biggest unknown:** hypotheses may be indistinguishable under available interventions. This improves experiment information, not necessarily watermark survival, and model-dependent information gain is not itself evidence that an explanation is true.

## RO11 — Reliability stress testing as a T3 search process

**Analogy:** adaptive stress testing of a simulator to locate rare failures. **Class/point:** R-full; **P6**. **State/action/feedback:** an attacker sees the current published carrier, previous allowed regeneration outputs, declared detector feedback and remaining fidelity/query budget; choose the next regeneration family, strength or stop. Feedback rewards removal under an independently specified attack-fidelity constraint. **Why it might beat non-RL:** history-dependent chains may expose a failure mechanism absent from any one-step grid; compare matched random chains, tree search and black-box optimization. **Biggest unknown:** success may merely reflect more distortion or detector access. Query access is a prospective threat amendment, not the original Q=0 setting; the generic RL regeneration attacker is already occupied by DRIFT.

## RO12 — A game-AI population that retains different exploits

**Analogy:** game-playing populations that preserve strategically distinct opponents rather than only the latest champion. **Class/point:** R-game; **P7/P6**. **State/action/feedback:** maintain training-only writer–attacker payoff vectors separately for T3 removal, T4 donor transfer and T5 wrong-instance acceptance. Choose opponents and learn bounded responses while preserving diversity of failure mechanisms. **Why it might beat non-RL:** training against only the strongest mean attacker can forget a less frequent but binding-critical exploit; compare exact finite-game mixtures, fixed diverse pools and double-oracle search. **Biggest unknown:** a meaningful response space may not exist, or diversity may only increase evaluation coverage. PSRO and generic adversarial training are occupied ingredients; any article claim would need a specific security payoff insight.

## RO13 — Redundancy allocation like a communication link

**Analogy:** communication-system rate adaptation and unequal error protection. **Class/point:** R-bandit; **P1/P9**, with a possible **P2** allocation action. **State/action/feedback:** use clean-source statistics to choose a fixed-payload redundancy profile across compatible carrier groups; learn which profile retains the original content code and clean quality after training channels. **Why it might beat non-RL:** source-dependent erasure patterns could favor different allocations over uniform redundancy or fixed unequal protection; compare coding-theory allocation and supervised selection. **Biggest unknown:** the writer has no acknowledgement from the future regeneration channel. The communication analogy cannot grant that information, and adding parity or splitting across incompatible carriers explicitly changes capacity, reader requirements and the comparison contract.

## RO14 — Randomized defense with an explicit observation game

**Analogy:** moving-target defense and game-theoretic randomized patrols. **Class/point:** R-game; **P7/P1**. **State/action/feedback:** choose a distribution over contract-compatible writer configurations before release; give the attacker only its declared public observations and queries; learn distributions against observed responses. Report both realized and expected constraints, retaining per-image clean quality. **Why it might beat non-RL:** no single attack may dominate all configurations when its available observations cannot fully identify them; compare an exact minimax mixture over the same menu. **Biggest unknown:** the published carrier may reveal its configuration, eliminating the proposed information advantage. Randomization is neither secrecy nor cryptographic ownership, and learned equilibrium adds value only if it enlarges or solves the response problem better.

## RO15 — Query planning for a registry verifier

**Analogy:** database query optimization with selective indexes and costly candidate verification. **Class/point:** R-full; **P10/P5**. **State/action/feedback:** observe a suspect, allowed registry metadata, current candidate set and previous descriptor results; choose the next permitted descriptor/index query or abstain. Learn retrieval coverage, query cost and valid final decision behavior on future disjoint data. **Why it might beat non-RL:** the best query order may depend on earlier candidate-set changes; compare fixed query plans and selectivity-based greedy planning. **Biggest unknown:** adaptive candidate selection can alter false-acceptance risk and hide retrieval misses. The trusted registry is explicit detector side information, and a fast wrong-instance match is not successful ownership verification.

## RO16 — Explore treatment differences, not easy survival labels

**Analogy:** adaptive experimental design for heterogeneous treatment effects. **Class/point:** R-bandit; **P8**, supporting a later **P1** selector. **State/action/feedback:** observe only prospective fresh-development context and uncertainty in pairwise configuration advantages; allocate the next randomized configuration comparison to learn where actions differ. Feedback is action-advantage prediction assessed on a separate fresh development set, not retrospective oracle improvement. **Why it might beat non-RL:** targeted exploration could avoid spending every observation on sources whose outcomes are predictable but whose best action is invariant; compare stratified random factorial allocation and non-RL active learning. **Biggest unknown:** exploitable treatment heterogeneity may not exist, and adaptive sampling can bias population claims. Propensities and inclusion rules must support the declared inference; existing Stage P successive halving remains unchanged.

## RO17 — Canary experiments for a changing regeneration service

**Analogy:** software canary releases and fault detection under changing operating conditions. **Class/point:** R-bandit; **P8**. **State/action/feedback:** in a prospective sequence of declared regenerator versions, track change uncertainty from fresh development sentinels; choose the next source stratum/configuration to query or trigger a separately governed reevaluation. Feedback is detection delay for a real efficacy change, with controlled false alarms and charged query cost. **Why it might beat non-RL:** change-sensitive allocation might expose a narrow new failure faster than a fixed regression suite; compare classical change detection with a fixed or information-based sampling plan. **Biggest unknown:** abrupt changes may be unobservable from the sentinel family. This supports maintenance of evidence, not robustness by itself, and does not authorize ongoing monitoring or any amendment to F6.

## RO18 — Allocate a batch budget like an inventory controller

**Analogy:** inventory management or job scheduling, where spending resources now changes what remains for later jobs. **Class/point:** R-full; **P8/P4**. **State/action/feedback:** expose the remaining batch budget, permitted clean-source features and partial writer progress for each source; choose which source receives the next refinement block, or stop it. Final feedback is prespecified all-attempted-source C/J performance with strict clean constraints. Refused or unfinished sources remain in the denominator. **Why it might beat non-RL:** a controller could trade uncertain future gains across sources, unlike identical per-source iteration counts; compare dynamic programming, index scheduling and learning-curve extrapolation. **Biggest unknown:** useful progress forecasts may require spending most of the budget first. This coupling is across sources, distinct from RO08's within-image allocation among quality constraints.

## RO19 — Learn the verifier's behavioral abstraction before forging

**Analogy:** active automata learning and protocol fuzzing, where queries reveal hidden decision structure. **Class/point:** R-full; **P6**, specifically T4/T5. **State/action/feedback:** with a prospectively declared query interface, build a belief over which public donor/recipient manipulations or look-alike candidate properties affect presence, decoded content and final acceptance. Choose a diagnostic query, an exploit attempt or stop; feedback includes only fields actually exposed by that interface. **Why it might beat non-RL:** discovering a reusable branch structure could guide later forgery queries more effectively than independent random proposals; compare grammar fuzzing, active model learning and black-box optimization. **Biggest unknown:** the real interface may reveal only a binary result, making the abstraction unidentifiable. No private clean residual, secret key, ground-truth identity label or internal score is silently granted to the attacker.

## RO20 — Counterexample-guided refinement of the surrogate contract

**Analogy:** counterexample-guided program synthesis and abstraction refinement. **Class/point:** R-game; **P7/P3**; the classical synthesis loop itself is an honest non-RL comparator. **State/action/feedback:** a writer proposes a bounded program using its current surrogate description; an adversary seeks a training-only native counterexample where the surrogate predicts preservation but C/J fails. The response must explain the witness with a changed feature, surrogate rule or policy, then seek a new witness. **Why it might beat non-RL:** learned counterexample selection could reveal systematic simulator blind spots that uniform augmentation rarely visits; compare classical counterexample search, robust optimization and fixed adversarial sampling. **Biggest unknown:** stochastic failures may not admit a compact, transferable abstraction. The output could be a falsified surrogate assumption rather than a stronger watermark; neither a finite witness set nor game convergence certifies arbitrary unseen channels.
