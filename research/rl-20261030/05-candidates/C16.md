# C16 — Candidate-aware registry query planning

The shared data, endpoint and cost definitions in [the author bundle](../ideas/sheets-systems.md) are part of this sheet. Final selection is in [the candidate table](../05-candidate-table.md).


**Class / points / raw IDs / analogy.** R-full, P10/P5; RT18, RS11, RO15. Outside analogy: database query optimization with selective indexes. Benefit type: registry verification efficiency and article evidence; direct defensive efficacy requires a separate matched-risk C/J improvement, not merely faster lookup.

**Mechanism and information.** State: suspect descriptor prefix, fixed enrolled-record roster, candidate set, past descriptor comparisons and remaining work. Action: acquire one of three frozen descriptor channels, inspect a candidate record, refine retrieval, or abstain/stop. Transition changes available comparisons and candidate membership, not registry contents or the image. Horizon three descriptor acquisitions and at most eight candidate inspections. Reward: correct exact-record attribution minus measured acquisition cost, subject to fixed coverage/error constraints. Sender enrollment and verifier access to original-record descriptors are explicit privileged side information. The registry may contain query-source reference records by design; their enrollment features are allowed, but evaluation outcomes and labels cannot train the policy. Competing records remain present; removing difficult rivals is prohibited.

**Strongest equal-total-budget alternatives.** Fixed ANN retrieval plus all-candidate verification; exhaustive comparison with cached descriptors; selectivity-based query planning; greedy entropy reduction; supervised cost-sensitive query prediction; random order; small finite enumeration of three-descriptor orders and fixed stopping policies. Same enrolled records, descriptors, metadata, cache, null roster, total fit/tune/evaluate/deploy budget and retrieval-recall accounting. Original-image access is granted equally. End-to-end reader gradient fitting, if added, is a separate charged component and receives a matched non-RL arm. A state-shuffled controller isolates history dependence.

**Necessary-condition evidence / effect.** N1/N2 hold structurally: record/descriptor queries are discrete and previous queries eliminate candidates. MEASURED context: the retained registry confirmation had false acceptance on operationally distinct look-alikes; this establishes a hard task, not a learnable query-order effect. JUDGMENT: descriptor disagreements could offer headroom, but N3/N5 and valid candidate-conditioned nulls are unknown. Prospective smallest worthwhile effect: >=30% total lookup-cost saving at the same exact-record recall and false-attribution operating point, or a >=10-point J/record-accuracy gain at equal cost and coverage. No effect is forecast as measured.

**Data, endpoints and error contract.** F supplies 72 query-source groups (24/24/24 train/calibrate/locked) and 16 additional independent distractor records, a fixed 88-record roster. Descriptor fitting uses only train groups; enrollment of later groups follows the frozen extraction rule. Operationally distinct T5 pairs require independent labels. Strict final clean PSNR >35, SSIM >.9, LPIPS <.1 remains mandatory for marked arms; fingerprint-only has no embedding but is an explicit comparator, not a free watermark success. Report C/J and exact record identity separately from retrieval recall, exact h, C0/wrong-owner, T4/T5, coverage and abstention. A new registry-assisted reader cannot borrow F5 FPR. Candidate-selection-aware error validity is **unidentified**, especially under roster growth or chosen malicious records; G5 is unknown.

**First decisive falsification / cost / kill.** L72 gives6.642h including reserve/setup. Complete three-channel candidate tables for C1, C0, clean and T4/T5 comprise `(864+864+72+144)×88×3=513,216` descriptor comparisons, plus extraction of query and enrolled features, policy fitting and calibration. Their cost `D` is **unmeasured**;1.4 seconds per entire F5 read is not a measured descriptor-pair cost. Total is `6.642 + 1.2D + H` hours, with `H` actual training/tuning; a2h H cap leaves D<=9.465h to complete under20h. Even at an illustrative .01/.1 seconds per comparison the pair arithmetic alone is1.426/14.256h, before feature extraction; these are sensitivity values, not timing estimates. **EXPENSIVE / completion within20h not established.** A strict20h resource cap permits only RESOURCE_STOP if inventory is incomplete. Kill if exhaustive available descriptors cannot separate legitimate and false records at the required coverage, if adaptive retrieval loses true records, or if fixed/greedy order matches the policy after total cost. This is a finite-roster falsification, not proof of valid rare-error control.

**Later evidence / cache.** At240 query groups plus64 fresh distractors, the same three-channel audit needs `(2880+2880+240+480)×304×3=5,909,760` comparisons. L240 contributes19.806h; full cost is `19.806+1.2D_full+H_full`, USD .551 times that quantity, and has no defensible numerical upper bound yet. All reader/descriptor training remains separately unmeasured. Cache fixed enrollment descriptors and published carriers; a query-plan change never warrants a rewrite. Equal-total-budget savings must include building the index and feature cache and predeclare how many future queries amortize them.

**Closest three inspected works and residual claim.** [DREW, §§3.3–4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02836v2) already combines ECC pointer decoding, conditional retrieval and fallback search. [Rel-Zero, §§3–5 and Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17531v2) already learns stable relational features with enrolled side information; its binomial assumption does not validate this adaptive roster. [Active Vision, §§3–5/Table3](https://minhhoai.net/papers/viewSel_cvpr20.pdf) learns state-dependent view selection, but for temporal recognition rather than records. TD03 I12 is the direct ambiguity-aware registry predecessor, and I08 supplies multiplicity obligations. Residual novelty is at most candidate-dependent query value with a valid attribution/cost comparison; generic registry pointers and abstention are occupied.

**Publishable claim, conditional.** Under a frozen roster and enrollment model, policy-dependent descriptor acquisition improves the measured correct-record/cost frontier beyond the strongest query planner at matched coverage and valid global risk. Until the error contract exists, the publishable scope is query efficiency with explicitly uncalibrated security implications.

**Steelman.** Two ambiguous records may differ only in a descriptor that is wasteful on easy queries, so earlier retrieval can genuinely determine the next informative operation. Caching creates a plausible low marginal-cost deployment regime.

**Hostile assessment.** Three descriptors have only six orders, and existing databases already optimize selectivity. A learned policy can appear strong by retrieving fewer hard records or trusting registry enrollment. The old near-copy failure may reflect indistinguishable observations that no query planner repairs; index speed cannot transform weak content binding into authentication.
