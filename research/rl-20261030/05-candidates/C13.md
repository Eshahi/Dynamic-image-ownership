# C13 — Detector-feedback disclosure game

The shared data, endpoint and cost definitions in [the author bundle](../ideas/sheets-security.md) are part of this sheet. Final selection is in [the candidate table](../05-candidate-table.md).


**Class / point / origins / analogy:** R-game; P5/P6/P7; RS12. Outside analogy: information-design and protocol-hardening games, where a service controls what each legitimate response reveals.

**Benefit type:** Security discovery, possible interface hardening and article evidence. WP6 A2 is **not applicable by design**: holding the internal reader fixed and restricting attacker information does not directly improve native C/J at unchanged access.

**State/action/transition/horizon/reward:** Defender commits to binary response, fixed coarse bins or declared full scores; an optional transcript-dependent disclosure policy is a new protocol. Attackers choose T4 probes/assemblies or unmodified T5 candidates from current transcript, Q=16. Each reply changes attacker knowledge; at most four committed T4 edits are allowed. Payoffs keep true legitimate verification, false exact-donor acceptance and query/latency costs separate. Train learned responses against each disclosure channel rather than testing only a score-trained attacker after removing scores.

**Information/leakage:** Internal reader thresholds and payload stay fixed. Disclosure format and bin edges are public/frozen from fit data; no hidden oracle label or score under a binary interface. Exact original donor q/h is fit reward/evaluator-only, not an exposed deployment field. Split source/scene/pair groups and attacker-training seeds; freeze protocol before evaluation. No F6/protected results or feedback-selected test challenges. Public owner IDs are not secrets.

**Necessary conditions:** N1 discrete communication decisions and N2 informative transcripts exist. Useful response learning, N4 reward, N5 deployment reuse and N8 fair interface-specific comparisons remain unproved. The simple three-protocol game may be solved without a learned defender; learned attackers must demonstrate added response power beyond non-RL search.

**Strongest equal-total-budget non-RL:** Fixed disclosure protocols plus exact empirical-game solution; beam/grammar search and finite-difference NES under scores, decision-only boundary/probe search under binary replies, matched public-surrogate PGD where allowed, and stratified random candidates. All receive the same per-channel information and total fit/tune/query/evaluation budget. Report channel changes separately from optimizer effects, and train a fresh best-response attack for every protocol. Fewer online calls cannot hide extra pretraining.

**Fresh data:** [Fresh-data contract](../ideas/fresh-data-contract.md),48 licensed groups24/8/16 with separately sampled T4 and unmodified T5 challenges. Custodian exclusion covers P/S/B/reserve/C and every consumed pool by ID/hash without reading protected identifiers here; related-image groups stay together. Missing human distinctness labels or inadequate targets make the corresponding endpoint unavailable.

**Security/fidelity:** Unchanged F5 C/J and q32+h32; P, exact h and C0/wrong-owner A separately recorded. Reference-assisted service variants explicitly declare trusted registry side information. T4 success requires exact donor q/record and binding on a different recipient; T5 remains unmodified. Strict clean quality is unchanged; attack-output fidelity is independently fixed. Every Q>0/adaptive/composed evaluation is a prospective access extension; finite Q hardening is not security against unlimited queries.

**First decisive falsification / kill:** On16 new groups, a restricted channel must prevent at least three otherwise valid exact-donor forgeries at equalQ16 while retaining all legitimate internal decisions and meeting a declared service task. Then compare learned versus strongest non-RL attackers within each channel. Kill the RL/game contribution if the result is just “fewer bits are revealed,” if fresh binary-trained attacks erase the advantage, if protocol changes deny legitimate service, or if an exact fixed-protocol table suffices. JUDGMENT three-group reduction is the worthwhile effect; no expected reduction is asserted.

**First cost:**48 cached F5 donor marks=1.63h. Three protocols×four attack procedures×900 calls=10,800, plus1,200 control/failed-call units=12,000, or7.30–12.10h at conservative native feedback cost. The900 per arm includes360 fit across three learner seeds,28 selection and512 evaluation =16×2 threat strata×Q16. Reserve3h for fitting, protocol bookkeeping, setup/retrieval:11.93–16.73h/USD6.57–9.22, cap20h. **Not EXPENSIVE for this bounded screen**; full learned attacker training remains unknown and excess is RESOURCE_STOP. F6's48 donor writes would be4.81–9.64h alone, not included.

**Later full evidence:**480 groups/120,000 calls and tenfold writing/reserve imply119–167h/USD66–92, before more training and wider query-budget curves. T5-only reads can be cheaper than2.19–3.63s; do not credit that saving until actual timer scopes are measured. Cached donors are shared, but every probe, failed request, service-side read and attacker pretraining call is charged.

**Three closest inspected works / delta:** [Policy-Driven Attack §§3.2–3.4,4.2](https://openreview.net/references/pdf?id=zQ8_-FYrn3) occupies learned hard-label queries. [HopSkipJump §§III–IV,V](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.02144v5) provides strong non-RL decision-only search. [SemBind §4.3,Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20310v1) occupies binding-aware adaptive challenges. Residual is an exact-attribution feedback-disclosure frontier with retrained access-matched opponents and legitimate-service accounting, not novelty in hiding scores. DRIFT already occupies generic RL regeneration.

**TD03 / publishable claim:** I05/I08 supply adversarial attribution and query accounting. Possible claim: “At a specified finite query budget, public score precision changes exact-attribution forgery risk after opponents adapt, without changing the internal verifier's genuine decisions.”

**Steelman:** Score precision can enable useful inference even when the final decision rule is unchanged; a reproducible access/forgery frontier could inform a real service. **Hostile:** The result may be conventional information restriction, not a learned defense; restricting feedback can obscure weaknesses from evaluation while leaving determined attackers effective.
