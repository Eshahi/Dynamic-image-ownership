# C04 — Adaptive probe acquisition and dual control before commitment

The shared data, endpoint and cost definitions in [the author bundle](../ideas/sheets-writers.md) are part of this sheet. Final selection is in [the candidate table](../05-candidate-table.md).


**Class, point, provenance and benefit.** R-full, P1/P3; RT02/RT05/RO04. Outside analogy: dual control and active system identification. Primary benefit is experiment/diagnostic or deployment-cost efficiency; direct defensive efficacy is a separate conditional claim. Learning which observation to buy is distinct from learning a better watermark.

**State/action/transition/reward/horizon.** Start from clean-source features and an uncertainty representation over the differential value of four registered writer configurations. At most two rounds choose one of two registered probes, perform a local writer update, restore a saved state, or commit to a configuration. Probe responses update information; updates change residual and future observations. Reward is terminal C/J under Q with separately reported observation cost; probe reward alone is not a surrogate for efficacy. Horizon includes at most two purchased observations and one commit.

**Information and leakage.** Probes are allowed source-model codec/noise responses or native checks on *known design channels*, not the unknowable future attacker. Native checking changes the deployment information/cost contract and is supplied to every control. A probe image must be written before probing; it is not a free post-regeneration feature. No excluded-family output is queried during fit, selection or deployment. D applies to all probe and writer sources; protected P/S/B/reserve/C identifiers/results remain unavailable.

**Evidence/conditions.** N2 is structurally satisfied because a response changes the information available for the second choice; N1 discrete observation selection also holds. Phase A's failed local transfer law and WP3's distinction between survival prediction and action advantage motivate measuring informative disagreement (E=2), not assuming that it exists. N3 differential predictability, N4 informative affordable probes, N5 repeated use, N6 transfer and N7 feasibility remain unresolved. A probe that only identifies an easy source does not pass the useful-action test.

**Strongest fair alternatives.** Fixed probe order, buy-all-probes and no-probe policies; greedy expected value of information; classical Bayesian experimental design/multi-fidelity BO; finite-horizon model-predictive control using the same learned response model; supervised action prediction from the same paid observations; tuned PGD/configuration search; random probe/configuration choice. Compare both equal total resources and a fully disclosed amortized deployment count. A privileged native-query RL policy cannot be compared only with a blind writer.

**Effect/claim target.** No numerical efficacy forecast. Efficiency worthwhile effect: **20% fewer charged probe/write seconds at matched prospective C/J and Q**, with training amortization declared; that is not Side A's defense-improvement event. To claim direct Side A efficacy instead, require +10 pp primary C beyond strongest equal-budget non-RL, J non-regression and Q. Publishable claim: the value of the second observation demonstrably depends on the first, and joint information/control planning beats equally informed greedy or classical design on fresh sources.

**First decisive falsification and kill.** Eight D sources: cache four terminal candidate marks and two separately written diagnostic probe marks per source, giving **48 writes**; evaluate the complete response/action table with two cells × three seeds. B covers **288 marked feedback, 48 C0, 288 extra wrong-owner, 48 clean rows and 96 T4/T5 units**, conservatively also checking diagnostic marks. Buy-all, no-probe, random, fixed-order and greedy policies are computed from this table; no additional terminal option is invented. A frozen shallow policy class may be cross-fitted for a diagnostic, never called efficacy. Kill the bounded route if even the full-information feasible action oracle has no useful headroom, or if the registered probe values cannot improve action prediction/cost over the fixed-order/greedy control on a separate check partition. With only eight sources, failure to detect a small effect is inconclusive; an exact finite-menu domination or resource violation is the decisive kill. Surviving is not proof of learnability.

**Cost and later evidence.** B(8,6,w)=**8.69–14.72 h** at unmeasured F6-like anchors, plus **at most 2 h reserved additional probe/feature overhead**, inside 20 h/$11.02. Charges include creating both probe marks; cached fixed candidates permit repeated feedback, but adaptive repair/rewrite paths later do not. Runtime or missing labels can cause RESOURCE_STOP; no full policy-learning cost is known. Later L applies with an additional `N_probe * measured_probe_cost` term, and each deployment pays selected probe images/readouts. The 86.38–156.76 h check-stage anchor scenario excludes this extra work. The illustrative 1,500-rewrite training scenario is a sensitivity case, not a sufficient sample count.

**Three closest inspected works.** [BASES, §§3.2–3.3/Algorithms 1–2 and §4.2/Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.03610v2) already adapts surrogate weights using target queries and hides substantial inner gradient work behind a low query count. [Active Domain Randomization, §§2–5.1/Algorithm 1, Appendices C/F/H](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/mehta20a/mehta20a.pdf) learns which simulator conditions are informative, with feasibility failures in overly hard regions. [Learning to Optimize, §§3.1–3.4/4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.01885) already uses history and gradients to control optimization; it does not establish probe value under a future inaccessible channel. Cards C04-08, C11-04, C06-01. None is evidence that a known-channel probe reveals an arbitrary excluded channel.

**TD03 comparison.** I03 already proposes finite reader-response forecasting and I06 stagewise rescue/erasure diagnostics. The residual is an *operational, paid, adaptive information-acquisition policy* affecting the next writer decision; a better forecast alone belongs to I03, not a new RL improvement.

**Steelman.** Full native observations are expensive but local probes can be cheap. If two source types require different second probes and opposite writer choices, learning the observation–action sequence could avoid many wasted rewrites while preserving the endpoint.

**Hostile reviewer.** The only informative probe may already cost a full mark and native regeneration. It then buys access, not algorithmic magic. Classical value-of-information planning is a very strong baseline, and known-channel responses may be irrelevant to the future foreign regenerator.

**Screens/verdict.** G1 PASS (N1/N2 acquisition structure); G2 PASS; G3 PASS (D); G4 PASS for measured contingent information value, novelty provisional; G5 PASS (paid native access disclosed, Q unchanged). **Provisionally eligible for SHORTLIST-CONDITIONAL; final selection deferred.** E=2 relevant mismatch diagnostic, M=2 genuine partial information, N=2 specific residual question, F=2 plausible capped falsifier with unmeasured probe costs, V=3 could change experimental/deployment decisions. Efficiency alone must not inflate direct-defense forecasts.
