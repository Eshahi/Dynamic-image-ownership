**Class, point, provenance and benefit.** R-full, P3; RT06/RO01/RS06. Outside analogy: compiler-pass ordering. Potential direct defensive efficacy and article mechanism evidence. The candidate asks whether **state-dependent order**, rather than an improved average surrogate mixture, matters.

**State/action/transition/reward/horizon.** Observe current residual, decoded/clean margins, previous surrogate responses, gradient alignment, named constraint slacks and remaining block budget. For the minimal test choose A or B at two block boundaries: A is a native-noise robustness-gradient block; B is a registered clean/content-constraint repair block using fixed fit-only T4/T5 counterexamples. AA/AB/BA/BB are the four paths. A chosen block changes the residual and thus the next gradient; terminal C/J is delayed reward with Q as hard feasibility. Full candidates may add family/timestep choices only after a new frozen action-set amendment, not by silently expanding after failure.

**Information and leakage.** All countersignals come from fresh fit sources and specified writer-side models. Deployment has the same declared local surrogate and generated counterexample access/cost, without an actual future attack response. Negative labels cannot be inferred from desired reward. Excluded-family outputs and protected F6/P/S/B/reserve/C data never update the scheduler; D applies. This changes a newly named writer controller, never frozen F6 or retained F5.

**Evidence/conditions.** N1 discrete block choice and N2 changing residual/gradient state are supported by WP1 D05–D11. MEASURED Phase A surrogate/native disagreement (near-orthogonal, oversized SDXL tangents) and WP3 margin/C mismatch support a relevant objective-control problem, giving E=2; they do **not** show beneficial order or differential action value. N4 reward usefulness, N5 amortization, N6 transfer and N7 secure feasibility remain open. Test whether order predicts terminal benefit beyond average gradient exposure and current losses.

**Strongest fair alternatives.** Tuned robust/EOT PGD with a fixed averaged objective; AdaEA-style gradient-conflict weighting; myopic worst-constraint selection; enumerated fixed schedules/beam search; supervised source/state-to-next-action prediction; uniform random blocks. Match total gradients by surrogate, native feedback, negative examples, stopping and quality evaluations. A state-shuffled controller and open-loop imitation are essential later. Extra training cannot be counted as zero because the writer itself is frozen.

**Effect/claim target.** JUDGMENT: effect is unknown; the full-study worthwhile target is +10 pp primary C, J non-regression and unchanged Q. Publishable claim: a preregistered action-order intervention and state ablation demonstrate a delayed constraint-recovery mechanism that survives strong matched robust gradients. A negative order result could constrain the role of RL; it would not refute all new surrogate objectives.

**First decisive falsification and kill.** Eight D sources × six paths (AA/AB/BA/BB, tuned fixed-mean PGD, gradient-conflict control), two cells × three seeds. Random and best-fixed schedules use the four-path table; all six paths receive the same full control inventory. **48 writes, 288 marked T3, 48 C0, 288 extra wrong-owner, 48 clean checks, 96 T4/T5 units.** Inspect registered intermediate states without changing final path outcomes. Kill the two-block mechanism if the complete feasible path oracle offers no primary-C improvement over the strongest simple schedule, if AB/BA effects vanish after counts/finalization are matched, or if a new false attribution/quality violation supplies the apparent gain. This is a necessary-condition falsifier; eight sources and no trained policy cannot substantiate +10 pp efficacy or generalization.

**Steelman.** A repair action may be unhelpful immediately but make a later robustness step safe; the reverse order may irreversibly spend quality slack. That is an actual credit-assignment problem if present, while native C/J can reveal a failure that local surrogate loss hides.

**Hostile reviewer.** Noncommutativity is common to ordinary optimization and does not imply that learned history is useful. An enumerated order or analytic gradient-conflict rule may capture it. The eight-source oracle is a tiny diagnostic and training could cost much more than any eventual gain.
