# C12 — Randomized writer commitments against an observing attacker

The shared data, endpoint and cost definitions in [the author bundle](../ideas/sheets-security.md) are part of this sheet. Final selection is in [the candidate table](../05-candidate-table.md).


**Class / point / origins / analogy:** R-game; P1/P7; RT14, RS05, RO14. Outside analogy: randomized patrols and moving-target defense with an explicit observation model.

**Benefit type:** Potential defensive efficacy from a declared policy mixture, security diagnosis and article evidence. WP6 A2 requires an actual T3 C/J gain with T4/T5 and quality preserved over the best finite-mixture/non-RL policy; an information-hiding thought experiment alone is not improvement. The current finite-menu formulation has no established incremental RL claim.

**State/action/transition/horizon/reward:** Defender observes source-only features and samples one of four contract-compatible configurations before publication. Attacker observes the pixels, public policy distribution and up toQ8 replies, predicts the realized configuration if useful, then chooses a permitted T3/T4/T5 response. One writer commitment followed by at most three attack actions is the episode; repeated training changes strategy distributions. Payoffs are separate genuine C/J and false donor/T5 acceptance under per-output quality constraints, not average watermark presence.

**Information/leakage:** Random seed is unobserved but no secrecy assumption is granted beyond the formal information set; explicitly compare attacker-blind, pixel-observing and oracle-revealed-action cases. Train the configuration classifier only on fit sources, charging its labels and computation; never supply true realized action to the primary evaluation attacker. Exact donor q/h and identity truth remain evaluator-only at deployment. F6 is excluded; no outcome-driven compatible menu selection.

**Necessary conditions:** N1 discrete commitment exists, but N2/M is weak: a four-action full-information mixture needs no RL. N3 source-dependent payoffs, N4 reward, N5 amortization, N6 transfer and especially N7 multiple secure compatible configurations remain unknown. The published image may disclose the random choice completely.

**Strongest equal-total-budget non-RL:** Exact minimax/constrained finite-game mixture, best single writer, tuned fixed mixture, double oracle with beam/NES or same-access gradient responses, and uniform random mixture. Compare a learned observer/attacker against supervised configuration identification plus non-RL best response with equal labels, queries and total fitting/tuning/evaluation compute. If the strategic set remains four fixed writers and a finite attack library, the exact solver is sufficient; do not rename its output RL.

**Fresh data:** [Fresh-data contract](../ideas/fresh-data-contract.md),48 licensed groups split24/8/16, with the four configurations fixed before results and every protected/consumed P/S/B/reserve/C pool excluded by custodian ID/hash checking. Group near-duplicates and donor/recipient relations before splits; all T4/T5 challenge labels are independently fixed and may remain unavailable.

**Security/fidelity:** Configurations must share declared q32+h32/reader semantics and satisfy strict clean quality individually; incompatible readers/payloads are separate methods, not hidden menu entries. Report C/J, P and exact h separately; T4 exact donor/record acceptance, unmodified T5 and C0/wrong-owner A cannot be averaged away. Attack fidelity and Q>0/chain expansion are explicit. Randomization is not cryptographic authentication or lawful ownership proof.

**First decisive falsification / kill:** Evaluate the observing-attacker regret gap on16 new source groups: require at least three additional valid T3 C/J survivals versus the best exact finite-mixture comparator and unchanged T4/T5/negative/quality outcomes, attributable to an actually learned response mechanism. Kill the hidden-choice premise if the pixel-observing attack matches the revealed-action attack; kill the RL route if the finite game attains the same result. JUDGMENT smallest worthwhile effect is three groups, not expected efficacy. A matrix alone can falsify the need for RL but cannot establish learned superiority.

**First cost:**48×4 cached F5-style marks=192 writes=6.51h.8,000 charged feedback units include4,608 initial payoff observations,2,400 observer/response fit and evaluation calls, and992 controls/failed calls:4.87–8.07h. Reserve4h for supervised/learned observer fitting, game solution, setup/retrieval:15.37–18.57h/USD8.47–10.23, cap20h. **Not EXPENSIVE for this bounded observation test**, but response fitting is unmeasured and may stop incomplete. F6 substitution would cost19.25–38.56h for192 writes alone and would be EXPENSIVE; it is excluded.

**Later full evidence:**480 groups/1,920 cached writes/80,000 observations plus40h reserve gives154–186h/USD85–103; any strategy expansion requiring newly trained writers adds full training and fresh writing costs. Reuse a fixed writer's carrier across attacks, never across changed writer configurations. Compatibility/data constraints may terminate the design before this scale.

**Three closest inspected works / delta:** [Double oracle §§2,4.2,5](https://cdn.aaai.org/ICML/2003/ICML03-071.pdf) already solves randomized robust commitments with response oracles. [PSRO §3 and §4](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/7007-a-unified-game-theoretic-approach-to-multiagent-reinforcement-learning.pdf) occupies learned mixtures/responses. [DRIFT §5.3 and Appendix E.2–E.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08213v1) includes blind family identification and adaptive removal. Residual is a bounded empirical measurement of whether the published carrier reveals the selected configuration and destroys a claimed randomization gain; generic randomized defense is occupied.

**TD03 / publishable claim:** I01 supplies writer options and I05 adversaries; neither establishes a hidden-action advantage. Possible claim: “Observable carrier cues erase, or leave, a measurable randomized-commitment advantage under a specified finite attack game.”

**Steelman:** If different secure configurations resist different attacks and are difficult to identify, a mixture may improve worst-case behavior. **Hostile:** The image can identify its configuration; even if it does not, solving a four-row payoff matrix already gives the mixture. A neural policy adds training cost without changing the information structure.

**Screens / provisional verdict:** G1 PASS discrete commitment premise; G2 PASS exact finite game and observing response; G3 PASS future contract, compatible menu unresolved; G4 PASS only for bounded observable-action evidence, no new RL mechanism; G5 PASS explicit information structure.
