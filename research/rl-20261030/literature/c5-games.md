### C05-01 — The Gaussian Watermarking Game

**Metadata.** Aaron S. Cohen and Amos Lapidoth. IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 48(6), 1639–1667, 2002. DOI: `10.1109/TIT.2002.1003844`. Canonical: [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2002.1003844). Inspected [MIT author manuscript](https://dsp-group.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/GaussianWatermarking.pdf), 73 PDF pages; manuscript revision date not established. Access: 2026-10-30. Status: `FULL_TEXT_INSPECTED_ONLINE`. Locators: introduction; §2.1–2.3, Theorems 2.1–2.3, especially PDF pp.12–16 and optimal-strategy explanation following Theorem 2.1.

**SOURCE.** Setting: post-hoc cover-sequence watermarking, abstract signals rather than a modern image benchmark; public and private decoders differ in cover access. RL class: comparator-not-RL, analytical zero-sum game. State/action/reward: RL state N/A; encoder chooses code, attacker chooses distortion-constrained channel; objective is reliable communication rate. Comparator: public versus private information and general versus additive-only attacks. Effect: no empirical effect size. Theorem 2.1 gives equal public/private capacities for i.i.d. zero-mean Gaussian cover under its almost-sure constraints. Theorem 2.2 gives zero achievable positive rate under the alternative expected-distortion formulation and stated cover assumptions. Theorem 2.3 shows restricting attacks to additive noise changes capacity. Limits: asymptotic Gaussian/quadratic model; no diffusion, image quality metrics, record binding or learned-policy experiment.

**JUDGMENT — pre-emption and relevance.** Minimax watermark design and attacker/decoder side-information distinctions are occupied ingredients. A proposal must specify the feasible attack set before claiming an equilibrium protects anything. Expected distortion cannot stand in for this project's per-image quality gate; the capacity theorem itself is not a measured ceiling for natural-image regeneration.

Limits: neural approximations/shared capacity and restricted game setting; learned exploitability against a population is not global security.

## Cluster synthesis — project judgments, not paper results

**Answer to the equal-budget question.** The inspected set does not establish that RL beats the simplest strong non-RL alternative for post-hoc content-bound image watermarking under diffusion regeneration. It establishes useful game formulations and examples of learned response populations in other domains. There are concrete reasons to test learning—state spaces too large for exact traversal, reusable response skills, and strategically diverse opponents—but none supplies the missing image result. FSP's small-game disadvantage and double oracle's non-RL efficiency make exact/finite alternatives substantive competitors. NeuPL gives a positive transfer example; its favorable update comparison does not price all payoff estimation and training.

**Occupied ingredients versus task novelty.** Encoder/decoder versus distortion-constrained attacker, robust false-positive control, iterative worst-case policy addition, historical opponent mixtures, neural response oracles, correlated meta-solvers and shared policy representations are all occupied. None of the twelve inspected papers evaluates the full conjunction of this project's post-hoc writer, registry/content binding, diffusion regeneration, strict per-image quality and T3/T4/T5 endpoints. That bounded observation is not a first-ever or absence claim; inaccessible classical papers and uninspected later follow-ups remain explicit.

**P1/P2/P3 implications.** P1 is a per-image budget/carrier/configuration decision from clean features. If it is one-shot and rewards are obtainable for a small menu, compare robust finite selection and a contextual predictor/bandit before a sequential RL policy. P2 allocates signal across regions/bands: compare constrained direct optimization, coordinate search and a finite allocation library; calling the objective a game does not change this baseline. P3 chooses surrogates/timesteps/transforms throughout the writer loop and is the most natural sequential candidate here. Its state must contain enough information for actions to change subsequent feasible progress; otherwise a fixed/diverse schedule or a bandit can perform the same job. PSRO could expand adversary/surrogate mixtures around any of the three, but the meta-solver is not evidence that its response oracle must use RL.

**Minimum fair future comparison.** Freeze feasible writer/attack policies, detector information, thresholds and total budget. Compare fixed diverse mixtures, finite payoff-table minimax/LP or double oracle, and non-RL response search before an RL oracle. Charge data collection, policy learning, meta-payoffs, failed seeds, tuning, evaluation exploiters and deployed inference. Report frozen-mixture performance and fresh-attacker performance separately. Approximate best responses give lower bounds on an attacker's attainable exploitation; failure to find a stronger attack is not an upper security bound.

**Transfer, quality and threats.** Separate training adversaries from an entire independent regenerator family. Keep hard clean-image quality and false-positive constraints outside a reward tradeoff that could silently relax them. Measure T3 owner/record recovery, T4 transplanted-mark rejection and T5 collision/near-duplicate rejection separately, with registry side information disclosed. A low empirical-game exploitability, continuous watermark MSE, poker payoff or cross-seed coordination score proves none of these endpoints. The actual watermark theories are especially useful for specifying assumptions and showing why arbitrary changes to distortion or observation models change the question being solved.
